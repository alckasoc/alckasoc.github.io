    Gemini 3.6 Flash and Mage-Flow Launches

    Two independent Tier 1 launches land in the window. Google DeepMind ships a coordinated agentic-Flash triad — Gemini 3.6 Flash (workhorse successor to 3.5 Flash), Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (fastest 3.5-class model at 350 output tok/s), and Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber (CodeMender-exclusive vulnerability-hunting derivative of 3.5 Flash). 3.6 Flash cuts output tokens 17% and output price to $7.50/M while jumping DeepSWE (37 → 49%), MLE Bench (49.7 → 63.9%), OSWorld-Verified (78.4 → 83.0%), and GDPval-AA v2 (1349 → 1421); 3.5 Flash-Lite at $0.30/$2.50 per million tokens beats 3 Flash on SWE-Bench Pro (54.2 vs 49.6%) and OSWorld-Verified (74.0 vs 65.1%); 3.5 Flash Cyber + CodeMender reaches CyberGym frontier at Flash economics and finds 55 unique confirmed V8 bugs vs 47 for mainline 3.5 Flash and 36 for Claude Opus 4.6 (including 10 caught by neither), with the CVR team producing a 100%-reliable RCE exploit that bypassed ASLR and W^X in 2 h. Microsoft ships the day’s second Tier 1: Mage-Flow, a compact 4B text-to-image + instruction-editing family built on Mage-VAE (one-step diffusion-style tokenizer distilled from FLUX.2-VAE, matching its reconstruction fidelity at 12.3×/22.3× fewer encode/decode MACs) and a Native-Resolution MMDiT with FlashAttention variable-length packing, per-sample 2D RoPE, and stack-level fused CUDA kernels (MFU 33.2 → 77.3%, 2.49× training speedup). Base, Diffusion-NFT-aligned, and 4-step Decoupled-DMD Turbo variants for both generation and editing; at 1024² on a single A100, Turbo generates in 0.59s, edits in 1.02s at 18–20 GB peak — matches or beats Qwen-Image 20B, Z-Image 6B, FLUX.2 32B, and FireRed-Image-Edit 20B. Tier 2: Meta AI’s GAMUT introduces a two-level meta-rubric benchmark for factual completeness in long-form multimodal grounded generation (1,813 wearable-imagery questions, 10 domains, expert-verified rubrics), with the best of 14 frontier models topping out at 58.7% (Gemini 3.1 Pro); Tencent Hunyuan’s SAT (Staleness-Adaptive Trust Region) contracts only the sign-selected endpoint of the PPO clip interval on high-mismatch tokens with proven local interval containment and pointwise pessimism relative to PPO, restoring AIME24 avg@8 to 34.79 at lag 8 in asynchronous MoE RL over Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base.

  • July 21, 2026

    RynnBrain 1.1 Launch

    Alibaba DAMO Academy ships the Tier 1 launch of the window: RynnBrain 1.1, a family of embodied foundation models at three scales (2B, 9B, 122B-A10B) built on Qwen3.5, extending RynnBrain 1.0 with contact-point prediction (compact (p, θ) action-anchor representation over 2.6M rendered grasp samples) and native 3D grounding (9D (cx, cy, cz, w, l, h, pitch, yaw, roll) box tokens for the 2B and 9B). All spatial outputs share a [0, 1000]-quantized autoregressive vocabulary. Post-training yields RynnBrain-VLA, a single-stream Diffusion Transformer flow-matching policy with a shared 81-D action space partitioned into body-part groups (Arm-Joint 14D, Arm-EEF 18D, Gripper 2D, Hand 40D, Torso 4D, Head 3D) driven by an embodiment mask, plus Real-Time Chunking (32-step chunks, re-inference every 5 steps, β = 10.0 action guidance) for smooth 200 Hz control. The 122B-A10B sets a new state-of-the-art on VSI-Bench (75.0), MMSI (52.0), RefSpatial-Bench (79.1), MindCube (89.6), and RynnBrain-Affordance (91.3) — leading every evaluated open-source and proprietary model, including Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5, and Tencent HY-Embodied 0.5. RynnBrain-VLA beats Qwen-based VLAs and representative generalists on Unitree G1, Astribot-S1, and Tianji-Wuji, with joint multi-task / multi-embodiment training gaining over per-task fine-tuning. Tier 2: Microsoft Research’s LLM-as-a-Coach retires scalar RL rewards on non-verifiable tasks in favor of textual experiential knowledge distilled from an LLM-as-a-Judge, conditions a teacher, and internalizes improvements via on-policy context distillation — beating rubric-based RL on held-out and unseen open-ended tasks and mitigating reward hacking under both self-feedback and proprietary-model feedback.

  • July 20, 2026

    Xiaomi-Robotics-1 Launch

    Xiaomi Robotics ships the Tier 1 launch of the window: Xiaomi-Robotics-1, a foundational VLA model at three scales (2.6B / 5.1B / 10.5B) pretrained on over 100,000 hours of UMI handheld-gripper trajectories under a two-stage recipe (embodiment-free pre-training on state-transition-captioned data + cross-embodiment post-training on ~10K hours of robot data). Mixture-of-Transformers couples a Qwen3-VL backbone to a matched-depth, half-hidden-size DiT that generates action chunks by flow-matching Euler integration; a Choice-Policies auxiliary head accelerates VLM convergence while action-token attention masking blocks the DiT copy-shortcut. New state-of-the-art of 57.4% on RoboCasa365 (prev best ABot-M0.6 at 46.6%), 20.07 on RoboDojo (prev 13.07), 74.5% on RoboCasa, 59.1% on VLABench. Downstream: 75% avg success on four novel dexterous / mobile tasks with <10 h/task, vs π0.5’s 40%. Tier 3: Alibaba Qwen previews Qwen3.8-Max at WAIC 2026 — a 2.4T-parameter multimodal model “second only to Fable 5” shipped without a benchmark table, active-parameter count, model card, or license.

  • July 19, 2026

    Robot Context Scaling, Streaming Video Framing, and Flow-Model RL

    No Tier 1 launch in the window. Three Tier 2 research papers from three frontier labs. NVIDIA GEAR’s RoboTTT integrates Test-Time Training fast weights into GR00T N1.7’s DiT action head and scales visuomotor context to 8K timesteps under a fixed GPU budget via sequence action forcing + truncated BPTT, unlocking one-shot imitation from human video, DAgger Distillation, and full completion of a five-minute ten-stage assembly task no baseline solves (+87% avg vs single-step GR00T N1.7). Alibaba Wan Team’s Wan-Streamer v0.3 reframes native-streaming generation as a world + event stream decomposition, widens the agent from listening to open-vocabulary behavior via a role-play chat format with parenthesized directives, and preserves v0.2’s 640×368 at 25 FPS with ~200 ms model-side latency. Tencent Hunyuan’s MeanFlowNFT is the first forward-process RL framework for MeanFlow generators: it uses the MeanFlow identity to construct an induced instantaneous-velocity predictor, applies the DiffusionNFT objective to that induced predictor, and preserves few-step MeanFlow sampling — 4-step MeanFlowNFT reaches VBench 84.33 on Wan2.1 1.3B, surpassing 50-step LongCat-Video RL 82.57.

  • July 17, 2026

    WanSong v1.0 Launch

    Alibaba's Wan Team ships the Tier 1 launch of the window: WanSong v1.0, a ~25B pure-diffusion song foundation model that directly outputs demixed vocal + BGM stems for multilingual songs up to 5 minutes in a single run. Hybrid-MMDit backbone with fully-shared AdaLN inherited from Wan2.1, a 1-D continuous VAE at compression 1024 (half of Stable Audio 2), a three-stage flow-matching pre-training curriculum (90s → 300s → SFT), and a two-stage DPO→ReFL RLHF post-training on three reward models (musicality, lyric accuracy, prompt alignment). PER 7.43% vs 22.80% Suno V5 / 12.7% Mureka V7.6 / 27.11% LeVo; tops every SongEval dimension and reaches a 5.49 musicality score vs Suno V5’s 4.18 on the WanSong-team 80k-song evaluator. Tier 2: Ant Group SearchOS-V1 replaces the implicit multi-agent scratchpad with a typed persistent state — Frontier Task queue, Evidence Graph, Coverage Map, Failure Memory — and drives sub-agents with a pipeline-parallel scheduler that refills freed slots against unresolved coverage gaps, leading all baselines on WideSearch and GISA.

    Ring-2.5-1T-Zero Launch

    Ant Group's inclusionAI ships the Tier 1 launch of the day: Ring-2.5-1T-Zero, a 1-trillion-parameter (63B activated) MoE reasoning model trained purely by zero-RL from Ling-2.5-1T-Base under a four-stage pipeline (clipped-IS reasoning elicitation → self-distillation reset → sample-level second-stage RL → tier-based adaptive-depth third-stage RL) with FP32 attention softmax + LM head over a BF16 body and hybrid MLA / Lightning-Attention CP. About

This repository hosts daily reviews of newly released papers and technical reports from frontier AI labs. Each review is generated by a scheduled agent that scans arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Hugging Face Daily Papers, X, the Emergent Mind newsletter, and other sources, then writes a structured summary covering methodology, evaluation, and results.

Only papers from a curated list of frontier labs are included. Reviews are deduplicated against prior entries — each paper appears at most once.
