    Muse Glimmer Launch

    One frontier launch anchors the 36-hour window. Meta Superintelligence Labs open-sources Muse Glimmer, a ~29.6B-parameter dense multimodal agentic model under Apache 2.0 — the first open-weight release from MSL since Muse Spark went closed in April, distilled from Muse Spark and tuned for on-device agent workflows. The architecture is a 52-layer causal transformer, hidden 6656, [Local, Local, Local, Global] repeating sliding-window attention (window 2048), gated attention, GQA 32:2, SwiGLU FFN 19,968, RoPE (θ=500,000) on local layers only, and a frozen ~1.8B ViT-G/14 Perception Encoder; context 131,072+, vocab 202,048, knowledge cutoff 2026-01-04. Three named contributions each with a mechanism explanation: (1) a three-phase distillation recipe (logit distillation on Muse Spark's outputs; agent-heavy longer-context mid-training with reasoning traces; SFT + on-policy distillation + RL across general / reasoning / coding / agentic) that transfers trajectory-shaped behavior rather than only next-token likelihood; (2) two 4-bit K-Quant variants — K-Quant-Dynamic (0.2% avg degradation, 32 GB envelope) and K-Quant-17GB (1.0%, 24 GB) — that ship as the shipped-and-validated artifact rather than leaving quantization to the community; (3) a DFlash block-diffusion drafter (block 16, sliding-window attention throughout, hooks at target layers {1,13,25,37,49}) that proposes 16 tokens per forward pass and is verified in parallel by the target, delivering 3.1× speedup on RTX 5090 (233.4 vs 74.9 tok/s), 1.8× on M5 Max, 1.5× on M4 Max at batch 1 greedy. Head-to-head vs Gemma4-31B (thinking) and Qwen3.6-27B (thinking): Glimmer sweeps general agentic (MCP Atlas 75.5, DeepSearch QA 74.6, τ3-Banking 23.5, WildClawBench 47.6, Gaia2 43.3), leads SWE-Bench Pro 51.2, SciCode 43.6, CharXiv 78.8, IFBench 77.0, AIME 2026 94.7, AA-LCR 80.0, BEAM-128K 65.1; cedes SWE-Bench Verified (Qwen 77.2 vs 76.0), Terminal-Bench 2.1, OSWorld-Verified, GDPVal-AA v2 to Qwen and GPQA / HLE to Gemma. Ships with a public Evaluation Methodology PDF; day-of vLLM/SGLang, llama.cpp/MLX/ExecuTorch in the following days.

    Turn-Level Credit Assignment for Agentic RL

    No new flagship model launches in the 36-hour window; a quiet Sunday cadence with a single Tier 2 headline. Tsinghua (with Zhejiang University and Meituan) posts AgentOPSD: Recursive Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning, the #1 Hugging Face paper of 2026-08-07, targeting a specific failure of on-policy self-distillation for agentic RL: the token-level teacher–student log-probability gap is not, on its own, sequential credit. Three named contributions each with a mechanism explanation: (1) formalize turn-level credit as the marginal revision of a Bayesian success belief in log-odds space, using the aggregated per-turn OPSD contrast as a proxy for the outcome-conditional Bayes factor; (2) a recursive belief update ck = γ ck−1 + ek, ℓk = logit(B0) + ck initialized from the clipped GRPO group success rate, so ΔBk ≈ Bk−1(1−Bk−1)(ek − (1−γ)ck−1) makes credit history-dependent and naturally suppressed once support saturates; (3) an outcome-aligned qk = sign(Aseq) ⋅ ΔBk and within-trajectory standardization feeding a bounded multiplier on the GRPO advantage — never reverses the outcome-supervised direction, requires neither a critic nor extra rollouts. Reaches 89.1% success on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-7B, beats GRPO and the strong self-distillation baselines (StepOPSD, SDAR, Skill-SD, RLSD, GRPO+OPSD) across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA at both 3B and 7B, and ablations attribute the gains specifically to turn-boundary aggregation aligned with environment transitions and to the history-dependent recursion (not to privileged teacher access alone). Code at github.com/ZethWang/AgentOPSD.

    Recursive Task Synthesis, World Rehearsal, and Delta Distillation

    No new flagship model launches from any frontier lab in the 36-hour window; four Tier 2 frontier-lab papers converge on training agents and multilingual reasoners when real environments and standard teacher signals are the bottleneck. Tencent HY LLM Frontier's Recursive Synthesis for Long-Horizon Terminal Tasks (Hugging Face #1 for 2026-08-06) proposes Recursive Synthetic Terminal Tasks (RST), a recursive verified pipeline that grows a seed pool of terminal-agent tasks fifteen rounds deep — 37,484 verified tasks at roughly $0.05 each, with DeepSeek-V4-Pro pass@4 collapsing from 90% at R1 to 2.5% at R15 without the recursion visibly saturating; downstream, agentic PPO on Qwen3.5-27B lifts Terminal-Bench 2 / Terminal-Bench Hard / Long-Horizon Terminal Bench by +20.0% / +41.2% / +21.9% relative over the base. NAVER AI Lab's OPD2 replaces the teacher's raw log-probability in on-policy distillation with the delta between the post-trained teacher and its base checkpoint — isolating what post-training actually added — and consistently beats vanilla OPD on Qwen3 multilingual math, with the largest gains in Korean and Japanese and a narrowed English–Korean gap; the ablation shows English-only OPD raises target-language scores but shifts responses toward English, so multilingual data is still required. ByteDance Seed's GST-Bench isolates global spatial awareness from local perception with a paired GST-Bench / GST-Bench-Local design over 6,790 minutes of synthetic video: the best zero-shot VLM reaches 42.68 against a human 79.08, but the same models do not show a comparable gap on the local split — the failure is cross-frame scene consolidation, not local perception.

    Rubric-Guided RL, Adversarial Self-Play, and Embodied VLMs

    No Tier 1 launch in the 36-hour window. Five Tier 2 frontier-lab papers converge on rubric-shaped credit assignment and adversarial training. ByteDance’s CoRT (#2 paper of the day on Hugging Face) keeps GRPO’s rollout, reward, and group-relative normalization unchanged but redistributes the response-level advantage across tokens using a policy-internal counterfactual: rescore the same sampled response under the criteria-free prompt x− = x, take the tokenwise log-probability contrast Δ_{i,t} = ℓ+_{i,t} − ℓ−_{i,t}, map to bounded response-normalized weights w_{i,t} constrained by (1/T_i) Σ_t w_{i,t} = 1, and substitute the shaped advantage Â_{i,t} = sg(w_{i,t}) · A_i inside the standard PPO-clipped GRPO surrogate — +4.4 pp average gain over matched response-level GRPO with no auxiliary discriminator. Alibaba’s Qwen Business Unit ships DecoEvo, which attacks the opposite side of the pipeline: co-evolve a solver skill and a rubric-generator skill under decoupled objectives, so the solver is updated by criterion-level feedback while the rubric-generator is judged by score-independent audits of requirement coverage and response discrimination — a generator that makes the rubric easier can no longer raise its own reward. 2.8–5.0% relative gain over SkillOpt across five benchmarks and three backbones under each benchmark’s official evaluation. Meta Superintelligence Labs’ Reinforcement Learning for Code Optimization (125-page report, FAIR authors including Chambon and Synnaeve) turns execution time into a learnable reward across three stages — a calibrated timing sandbox (DMC-Optim), an offline configuration simulator that selects promising correctness+speed compositions before any RL is run, and a noise-robust GRPO adaptation — pushing strict top-50% pass@1 from 18.0% → 31.3% on Qwen 2.5 7B and 30.7% → 50.4% on CWM 32B, with +125% relative at top-30% and about half the human rate of complexity-class improvements (14% vs. 28%) on LeetCode-style problems. OpenAI’s GPT-Red scales automated red-teaming self-play to the compute of a frontier RL post-training run — “the single-largest LLM safety training run ever documented” — training the attacker against a simultaneously-updating population of defender agents so attacks have to generalize across the population; the resulting model reliably breaks all OpenAI models up through GPT-5.5, finds more successful prompt-injection attacks than human red-teamers, and transfers to held-out environments, defenders, and harnesses. It was used to adversarially train GPT-5.6. Meta’s HumanCLAW (#3 paper of the day) closes with an embodied-VLM benchmark that decouples action decision from motor execution: a harnessed VLM emits atomic skill commands, the harness executes them as sub-second full-body physical motion, and only the decisions are scored. Nine SOTA VLMs on 1,218 long-horizon egocentric find-navigate-interact episodes across 41 indoor scenes; best model 16.8% success. Recognition is not the bottleneck — the models lose track of their own body.

    Mage-VL and Shieldstral Launches

    Two Tier 1 launches lead the 36-hour window. Microsoft publishes the Mage-VL technical report and drops the 4B checkpoint on Hugging Face — a codec-native streaming multimodal foundation model built around Mage-ViT, a 24-layer / 1024-dim ViT trained from scratch on 560M unlabeled images and 100M unlabeled video frames whose codec-driven patchifier keeps all I-frame patches and selects the top-k P-frame patches by an importance tensor S built from HEVC/H.265 motion vectors + residual energy (or DCVC-RT’s learned probability model), cutting visual token consumption by ~75% at a fixed 4,096-token budget for a 64-frame clip. On top, a bio-inspired dual-system stack pairs a lightweight System 1 cognition gate that decides when to respond over rolling codec windows with a frozen System 2 causal decoder (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) that generates the response, trained via a five-stage progressive curriculum ending in Stage-5 gate-only fine-tuning on 3.35M streaming samples.     Kimi K3 Open Frontier Launch

    One Tier 1 launch dominates the window: Moonshot AI publishes the Kimi K3 technical report and drops full open weights on Hugging Face — a 2.8T-total / 104B-activated MoE with native multimodal input, a 1M-token context, and a joint algorithm-system co-design across three named contributions. About

This repository hosts daily reviews of newly released papers and technical reports from frontier AI labs. Each review is generated by a scheduled agent that scans arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Hugging Face Daily Papers, X, the Emergent Mind newsletter, and other sources, then writes a structured summary covering methodology, evaluation, and results.

Only papers from a curated list of frontier labs are included. Reviews are deduplicated against prior entries — each paper appears at most once.
