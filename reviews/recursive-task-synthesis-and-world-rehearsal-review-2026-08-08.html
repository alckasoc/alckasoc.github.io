GST-Bench: Can VLMs Develop Global Spatial Awareness from Video?

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-06 VLMs · Spatial reasoning · Video · Benchmarks

Overview

  • ByteDance Seed introduces the Global-Spatial-Temporal Benchmark (GST-Bench), a VQA benchmark for global spatial intelligence in video understanding — the ability to consolidate a continuous long-horizon visual stream into a globally consistent scene representation, then answer questions from viewpoints unseen in the input.
  • Ships alongside a diagnostic split GST-Bench-Local that holds the task format constant while restricting the required reasoning to a single viewpoint, plus a complementary training set GST-Train.

Methodology

  • Problem the benchmark isolates. Existing spatial benchmarks score models on local perception from a single or a small number of viewpoints. A VLM can be very strong on local questions and still fail to build a scene-level map from a long video — that failure was previously invisible.
  • Mechanism. Human-verified VQA over 6,790 minutes of synthetically generated video. Two capabilities are stressed: (1) spatial inference from novel viewpoints unseen in the input video, and (2) mapping egocentric observations onto a global top-down image of the same environment. Because the video is synthetic, geometry and camera trajectory are ground-truth and the "novel-viewpoint" query is well-posed.
  • Why the local split matters. GST-Bench-Local uses the same question templates but restricts required evidence to one viewpoint. If a model scores well on GST-Bench-Local but poorly on GST-Bench, the missing capability is not perception but cross-frame consolidation. This design converts a single benchmark score into an interpretable diagnostic.

Evaluation & Results

  • 22 SOTA VLMs evaluated zero-shot. Best model scores 42.68; human score is 79.08 — a ~36-point gap.
  • Same models do not show a comparable gap on GST-Bench-Local under the identical task formulation, indicating that local spatial understanding is broadly present.
  • The delta between GST-Bench-Local and GST-Bench is the authors' proposed measure of a model's global-scene-consolidation deficit.
  • GST-Train is released as a complementary resource to close that gap.

Other

  • All video is synthetically generated, so the benchmark inherits its own domain-shift caveat — a VLM that solves GST-Bench may still fail on real-world video of the same task format. The paper does not claim otherwise.

On-Policy Delta Distillation for Multilingual Math Reasoning

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-06 Distillation · Post-training · Multilingual · Math reasoning

Overview

  • NAVER AI Lab (Byeongho Heo, Jaehui Hwang, Sangdoo Yun, Dongyoon Han) studies On-Policy Distillation (OPD) in multilingual settings and proposes OPD2 (On-Policy Delta Distillation), which replaces the teacher's raw log-probability with the delta between the post-trained teacher and its base checkpoint as the learning signal.
  • Framed as a post-training alternative to RL for the specific regime where the teacher is a lab's own post-trained model and the student is trained on-policy on the same architecture family (Qwen3 here).

Methodology

  • Problem. Vanilla OPD supervises student-sampled tokens with the teacher's log-probability. In a multilingual setting, most of that log-probability mass encodes shared linguistic priors that the student already inherits from its own pre-training. The specifically-learned post-training capability is a small perturbation on top — and it gets drowned out.
  • Mechanism — the delta signal. Let πT be the post-trained teacher and πT,base its own base checkpoint (i.e., the pre-post-training checkpoint of the same model). OPD2 supervises the student with log πT(y|x) − log πT,base(y|x) instead of log πT(y|x). Everything else in the OPD loop is unchanged: student samples on-policy, per-token divergence against the (delta) signal, standard optimizer.
  • Why. The delta strips out what the teacher already knew before post-training and keeps only what post-training added. In English, where teacher and base are both strong, this changes little. In Korean and Japanese, where the base is weaker and the post-training capability is a much larger fraction of the total probability mass, the delta is where the useful signal lives — and prior OPD was dominated by base-model bias that shifted student responses toward English.

Evaluation & Results

  • Backbone: Qwen3. Domain: mathematical reasoning in English, Korean, Japanese.
  • OPD2 consistently outperforms vanilla OPD across all three languages, with the largest gains in Korean and Japanese; the English–Korean performance gap generally narrows.
  • Ablation: English-only OPD raises Korean and Japanese scores but shifts responses toward English, defeating the point. Multilingual data is required to preserve target-language responses even with the delta signal.

Ablations

  • Head-to-head against selective OPD baselines (confidence-, information-, or learnability-prioritized token selection).
  • Language-mix ablations that trade off English proficiency preservation against Korean/Japanese lift.

Other

  • 9 pages, 3 figures, 10 tables. CC BY 4.0.
  • Directly applicable to any lab whose flagship post-training pipeline already produces both a base and post-trained checkpoint (i.e., all of them).

Recursive Synthesis for Long-Horizon Terminal Tasks

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-05 Agents · Terminal · Data synthesis · Agentic RL

Overview

  • Tencent HY LLM Frontier, with University of Georgia, UMD, Penn, Minnesota, Indiana, NUS, and PolyU, proposes Recursive Synthetic Terminal Tasks (RST): a recursive verified pipeline that grows a seed pool of long-horizon terminal-agent tasks — instruction, sandbox environment, reference solution, and verifier — fifteen rounds deep while keeping all four mutually consistent.
  • Ranked #1 on Hugging Face Daily Papers for 2026-08-06 (212 upvotes). Code, project site, and the full Recursive Task Synthesis collection released on Hugging Face.

Methodology

  • Problem. A useful terminal-agent training task requires four consistent artifacts — an instruction, a runnable workspace, a reference solution, and a verifier that unambiguously grades success. Human authoring costs hundreds to thousands of dollars per task. Direct one-shot LLM generation breaks the consistency: the solution stops passing the verifier, or the verifier stops matching the instruction. Neither scales to the difficulty regime where current agents actually still fail.
  • Mechanism — the recursive verified loop. Starting from a diverse seed pool (bootstrapped from 639 human-verified tasks), each round: (1) select a target from the seed pool under a diversity cap; (2) grow-solution-then-align — extend the reference solution with additional workflow steps using DeepSeek-V4-Pro, then rewrite the verifier and instruction to match the new solution; (3) run local generation-quality filters and a static preflight; (4) execute in a fresh sandbox against the rewritten verifier as an oracle; (5) accepted tasks join the seed pool and are eligible seeds for the next round. Fifteen rounds total.
  • Why it works. The verifier acts as an inductive filter that would kill any inconsistency between instruction, environment, and solution — one-shot LLM generation has no such filter. The recursion converts solver pass-rate on generated tasks (which drops as tasks get harder) into synthesis yield (which stays stable because the verifier only accepts internally consistent tasks). This decouples generation difficulty from validation difficulty.

Evaluation & Results

  • Synthesis scale and cost. 37,484 verified terminal-agent tasks across 15 rounds from 639 seeds. Cost roughly $0.05 per synthesized task — four orders of magnitude cheaper than human authoring.
  • Difficulty climb. Median reference-solution length grows from 67 to 374 lines (5.6×); median executed commands from 40 to 244 (6.1×); median instruction length only grows 1.4×, i.e., the tasks get harder without becoming verbose.
  • Solver collapse. Under the strictest partial-credit threshold, DeepSeek-V4-Pro pass@4 drops from 90% at R1 to 2.5% at R15; pass rate drops from 72% to 4% for DeepSeek-V4-Pro and 72.2% to 7% for GPT-5.6-sol from seed to R15.
  • SFT training utility. Rejection-sampled Qwen3.5 trajectories on the synthesized tasks, used for SFT, improve Qwen3.5-27B and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B by up to 10 points on Terminal-Bench 2, Terminal-Bench Hard, and Long-Horizon Terminal Bench.
  • Agentic PPO. Lifts Qwen3.5-27B further to 49.44% on Terminal-Bench 2, 32.00% on Terminal-Bench Hard, 22.07% on Long-Horizon Terminal Bench — relative gains of 20.0%, 41.2%, and 21.9% over the base model. Absolute deltas from SFT baseline: +8.24%, +9.33%, +3.97%.
  • No ceiling after 15 rounds. Synthesis yield and validation rates remain stable as difficulty keeps climbing — the recursion is not visibly capped by RST's own dynamics.

Other

  • DeepSeek-V4-Pro is the synthesis LLM; Qwen3.5 is the trained student; both DeepSeek-V4-Pro and GPT-5.6-sol are used as evaluation solvers.
  • Public collection on Hugging Face; project site at zhongzhi660.github.io/recursive-verified-synthesis-site.

EnvACE: Internalizing Environment Dynamics via World Rehearsal for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-06 Agentic RL · World models · Tool use · GRPO

Overview

  • Tencent (Yasheng Wang, Yan Xu), SJTU (Weinan Zhang, Weiwen Liu), CUHK (Xingshan Zeng), and collaborators propose EnvACE, an agentic-RL method that removes external environment interaction from training and rehearses the environment inside the policy.
  • Ships with a two-role training objective (Role-Wise GRPO) and a test-time private rehearsal mechanism.
  • Code: github.com/Within-yao/EnvACE.

Methodology

  • Problem. Agentic-RL training for long-horizon tool use depends on either (a) real executable environments — costly to build and verify, hard to scale — or (b) external LLM simulators, which are cheaper but produce inaccurate or inconsistent responses and still need real-environment grounding. In both cases the world-modeling capability lives outside the acting policy, which never has to learn how the environment responds.
  • Mechanism — world rehearsal. A single policy plays two roles per turn. First it emits a tool call as the actor; then, without querying any external system, it plays the environment and rehearses the response that tool call would induce; then it conditions the next action on the rehearsed response. A trajectory becomes a self-unfolded process rather than a dialogue with an external system. Both roles are jointly optimized end-to-end under a task-success reward using Role-Wise GRPO — the group-relative normalization is computed per role so the actor is not penalized for a bad rehearsal and vice-versa.
  • Test-time scaling. Because the same policy is now an internal world model, at inference it can privately rehearse a candidate action before committing to a real execution — extra rehearsal-budget compute converts to accuracy without additional external interaction.
  • Why it works. Prior work either consumes externally supplied observations (leaving env-modeling to the sandbox) or predicts them as an auxiliary loss (retaining external ground truth). EnvACE absorbs the simulator into the policy's parameters — the policy is forced to be consistent between its own actor and its own rehearsal because both are graded on the same task-success reward. That constraint is what turns rehearsal from imagination into a usable world model.

Evaluation & Results

  • Benchmarks: BFCL-v4, τ2-Bench, VitaBench, and FinMCP-Bench. EnvACE outperforms environment-scaling baselines in the overall evaluation and shows strong transferability across the four benchmarks.
  • Controlled studies show world rehearsal consistently improves policy learning across model scales — the gain is not a small-model artifact.
  • Test-time rehearsal budget yields further gains under moderate budgets, without any additional external interaction.
  • Training-dynamics analysis shows that both the actor and rehearsal-role log-likelihoods co-evolve, and that ablating the rehearsal role collapses performance back to the baseline — the two roles are causally coupled.

Ablations

  • World rehearsal on/off: removing the rehearsal role reverts EnvACE to a real-environment-only agentic-RL baseline; gains disappear.
  • Rehearsal-budget sweep at test time: monotonic improvement up to a moderate budget, then saturates.
  • Scale sweep: rehearsal-driven gains persist across model sizes tested.

Other

  • Task-success rewards are the only supervision; no per-turn dense reward and no critic. The method sits inside the same GRPO-family loop that has become the default for post-training agentic policies.
  • Positions itself alongside prior world-model-for-agents work (Qwen-AgentWorld, self-play world-model finetuning) — the distinction is that here the world model is not a separately trained artifact but a role the policy plays under a shared reward.
