Alibaba

Video = World + Event Stream (Wan-Streamer v0.3)

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-07-16 Streaming video · Real-time interaction · World models · VLA

Overview

  • Authors: Lianghua Huang, Zhi-Fan Wu, Yupeng Shi, Wei Wang, et al. Affiliation: Wan Team, Alibaba Group. Same group that shipped Wan-Streamer v0.1 (native-streaming end-to-end audio-visual interaction), Wan-Streamer v0.2 (higher resolution, Ulysses-style context-parallel performer, covered in the July 8 review), and WanSong v1.0 (covered in the July 17 review).
  • The paper is the launch of Wan-Streamer v0.3, but it is deliberately a conceptual version bump. It leaves the modeling contract, serving topology, and operating point of v0.2 intact and instead reframes the pretraining objective so the same architecture can serve a broader family of real-time downstream tasks.
  • Central claim: any video can be read as a persistent world plus a time-varying event stream. Pretraining on that decomposition over ordinary internet video defines a general-purpose objective — given a world and streaming input, predict the next event unit and its audio-visual realization — that can be specialized to real-time interaction, roaming/first-person world-model control, embodied manipulation, and other streaming tasks. This paper studies only the real-time full-duplex audio-visual interaction specialization.
  • Downstream expansion: v0.1/v0.2 agents mostly spoke and showed visible listening behavior; v0.3 opens the agent’s event stream to open-vocabulary behavior described in natural language via a role-play chat format that interleaves spoken words with parenthesized behavior directives.

Methodology

  • World-event decomposition (central named contribution). Problem. Prior versions treat one target application — the talking agent — as the training objective, folding persistent context (scene, character identity, ambient sound, voice timbre) into the causal history alongside per-frame changes. That entanglement (i) forces re-encoding of unchanging conditioning at every unit and (ii) ties the pretraining objective to one downstream interface. Mechanism. The world is represented as a flexible structured record W = {w_j}_{j∈S(W)} whose schema depends on the clip / task; fields typically group visual context (medium, environment, layout), acoustic context (ambient sound, voice characteristics), and character records (identity, appearance, persona, visibility). Each event is a time-localized record e_k = (τ_k, c_k, d_k) where τ_k is its active interval, d_k is a free-form language description of the change, and c_k associates the event with a character in W or explicitly marks it as character-independent. A video is W with its {e_k} stream unrolled; audio-visual pixels are produced by the causal decoders inherited from v0.1. Why it works. The world is tokenized and prefilled once before streaming, so long, high-value context (e.g. a full character bible) is amortized over the whole interaction rather than re-encoded per unit; naming the persistent context first-class also decouples the training objective from any single downstream interface, so the same pretrained weights specialize to roaming, embodied manipulation, or interaction by only changing what plays the roles of W and the driving input x_≤k.
  • Streaming world-event factorization. The model learns p_θ(e_{1:K} | W, x_{1:K}) = ∏_k p_θ(e_k | W, x_≤k, e_{<k}). The v0.1 autoregressive factorization is the special case where W is folded into the history and x is the user observation. Discrete parts of e_k (language, and now behavior directives) train with next-token prediction; continuous audio and video latents train with conditional flow matching under the same clean context, so speech, motion, appearance, and scene evolution are denoised as one coupled response and then committed back into the history for the next unit.
  • Free-form behavior in a role-play format. Problem. Prior interactive video agents pick an action grammar upfront (w/a/s/d roaming moves, fixed emotion labels, listening/idle motion) and cannot express behaviors outside it. Mechanism. The agent’s language stream interleaves spoken words with parenthesized behavior directives — e.g. (reaches into the grass and picks up a green leaf) “Look what I found” — and the parenthesized part can be any behavior expressible in language. Because behavior directives and spoken words share one token stream, their timing, ordering, and interleaving with speech are learned jointly rather than scheduled by an external controller. Directives are short language tokens, so they add negligible cost to the token-causal path. Why it works. Beats prior fixed-vocabulary agents on expressive range without adding a new latency-critical path, because behavior conditions the same audio-video decoders as the language stream already did.
  • Serving topology. Inherited unchanged from v0.2: thinker + Ulysses context-parallel performer. This is the source of the preserved operating point.

Evaluation & Results

  • Operating point (preserved from v0.2). 640×368 video at 25 FPS, 160 ms streaming unit, ~200 ms model-side response latency, ~550 ms total interaction latency under a 350 ms bidirectional network budget.
  • The paper reports only qualitative behavior observations for v0.3: in generated 640×368 conversations, the agent speaks while performing open-vocabulary behavior in real time; behavior directives are realized as coherent facial expressions, posture changes, responses to sounds, and interactions with nearby objects that remain synchronized with speech and grounded in the configured scene and character. Between explicit behavior events, the agent maintains stable idle and listening behavior.
  • No new quantitative benchmark is published in v0.3; the paper explicitly leaves systematic evaluation of roaming, embodied manipulation, and other downstream specializations to future work.

NVIDIA

RoboTTT: Context Scaling for Robot Policies

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-07-16 Robot foundation models · Test-Time Training · VLA · Long context

Overview

  • Authors: Yunfan Jiang, Yevgen Chebotar, Ruijie Zheng, Fengyuan Hu, Yunhao Ge, Jimmy Wu, Tianyuan Dai, Scott Reed, Li Fei-Fei, Yuke Zhu, Linxi “Jim” Fan. Affiliations: NVIDIA (GEAR lab), Stanford University, UT Austin. Equal-advising senior authors: Fei-Fei, Zhu, Fan.
  • Central claim: recent robot foundation models (Physical Intelligence π0.5, Figure AI Helix, and the GR00T N-series) operate on single-step or short-history (2–8 frame) visuomotor context. RoboTTT scales pretraining visuomotor context to 8K timesteps — roughly 4 minutes at 30 Hz control — without growing inference latency, and shows this unlocks new closed-loop capabilities that emerge only after context length is scaled.
  • Backbone: RoboTTT is instantiated on top of GR00T N1.7. It adds a TTT layer after each of the 16 DiT action-head layers; each fast model is a two-layer MLP. Pretraining uses a mixture of tabletop bimanual robot data and egocentric human data, gradually increasing pretraining context length to the target (e.g. 8K) for 30K steps on 16×NVIDIA GB200 GPUs; post-training runs on each downstream task at 1K context length for 20K steps.
  • Benchmark: three long-horizon, multi-stage bimanual assembly tasks — Pup Go Car (toy vehicle assembly, ~2 min episodes), Circuit (80 assembly configurations specified by language prompt or a one-shot human video demo, ~1 min episodes; trained on 20 configs, tested on 60), Gear Bot (10-stage, ~5 min episodes — the longest-horizon task).

Methodology

  • Test-Time Training layers in the DiT action head (central named contribution). Problem. Long visuomotor context has to solve three coupled requirements: encode long histories with sufficient capacity, effectively exploit the conditioned context, and keep per-step inference cost constant. Full-attention with a KV cache satisfies capacity but grows with history; RNNs (LSTM) keep inference constant but under-scale; naive frame concatenation introduces spurious correlations. Mechanism. The input to the DiT is a robot trajectory [R_t, Φ_t, q_t, Ã_t] repeated across T timesteps, where Φ_t are VL tokens from the VLM, q_t the proprioception token, Ã_t the noised action tokens, and R_t are N=16 learned register tokens prepended per timestep. Attention layers operate on the per-timestep tokens (R_t, q_t, Ã_t) and cross-attend to Φ_t; per-timestep attention outputs are then concatenated along time and passed through TTT layers whose fast weights W_t are updated inside the layer by W_t ← W_{t-1} - η∇_Wℓ​FW(f_{W_{t-1}}(K_t), V_t) with an MSE fast-weight loss, and whose apply step computes O_t = f_{W_t}(Q_t). VL tokens Φ are not sent through TTT directly for compute efficiency; the smaller register token pool carries VL information across time. Why it works. Fast weights parameterize a nonlinear MLP that is (i) more expressive than the vector-valued state of Gated DeltaNet, (ii) meta-learned end-to-end so the outer task loss shapes the fast-weight initialization W_0 and update dynamics, and (iii) constant-cost at inference. Ablations show the TTT-linear variant is 27% worse than the MLP fast model, echoing prior TTT results in language.
  • Gating for preserving pretrained capabilities. A learned per-layer vector α ∈ ℝd is initialized near zero (0.001) and gates the TTT contribution: O = tanh(α) ⊙ O_TTT + O_attn. This keeps the TTT signal small at the start of training and lets the model gradually turn it up as it learns to use it, without overwriting the base VLA capabilities of GR00T N1.7.
  • Sequence action forcing (named contribution). Problem. Sequence training with a flow-matching action head naively shares one noise level τ across the whole training sequence (“full-sequence diffusion”). That makes entire sequences uniformly easy (low noise) or uniformly hard (high noise) to learn and destabilizes training. Mechanism. Sample the flow-matching timestep τ_t independently for each action chunk in the sequence: τ_t = s(1-u), u ~ Beta(1.5, 1), s = 0.999. The per-timestep loss regresses v_θ(Φ_t, A_t^{τ_t}, q_t; W_{t-1}) against A_t - ε. Why. The ablation shows removing sequence action forcing collapses closed-loop performance: the resulting policy’s motions are inaccurate enough that the robot cannot make meaningful progress on Pup Go Car. Beats naive full-sequence diffusion echoing findings in image-sequence generation work by Chen et al.
  • Truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT) with carried fast weights. Problem. Full BPTT on 8K-timestep sequences stores activations for every timestep; GPU memory grows with sequence length and blows past a fixed budget. Mechanism. The training sequence is split into segments; gradients flow only within each segment; fast weights carry across segment boundaries with their gradients detached. So TTT continues over the whole sequence while GPU memory is set by the segment length, not the sequence length. The fast weight initialization W_0 still receives gradients through the first segment. Why. Decouples the training compute budget from the target context length, so 8K pretraining context is trainable on the same hardware as 1K.
  • DAgger Distillation (named contribution). Problem. Standard DAgger fine-tunes on the human corrections and discards the suboptimal robot actions that preceded them. Those suboptimal robot actions carry no imitation signal (the paper verifies: fine-tuning GR00T N1.7 on the full trajectories, robot actions included, performs identically to corrections alone — 57% for both). Mechanism. During sequence training, all executed actions — including the suboptimal robot actions themselves — update the TTT fast weights, but the flow-matching loss is masked to the human corrections only. Why. The failure-to-correction mapping is distilled into the fast weights: the model learns to produce corrections in response to failures rather than imitate corrections in isolation. Beats standard DAgger fine-tuning (+13% avg on the sequence models) with a further +33% avg improvement over base policies (+36% for RoboTTT, +29% for GDN), from the same DAgger data.
  • One-shot imitation from in-context human video. Human video demonstration ξvideo is paired with a robot trajectory ξrobot of the same task. The video sequence updates the fast weights only (its flow-matching loss is masked), while the action loss is computed on the robot trajectory conditioned on those updated fast weights. At test time, conditioning on a single human video of an unseen task configuration yields one-shot imitation.

Evaluation & Results

  • Main long-horizon evaluation. Task completion score averaged over Pup Go Car, Circuit, and Gear Bot: RoboTTT 79%, GR00T N1.7 (single-step) 42% (RoboTTT is +87% higher), GR00T N1.7 Hist. (one history frame) 45.6%, GDN (RoboTTT’s TTT layers replaced with Gated DeltaNet) 56% (RoboTTT is +41% higher).
  • Gear Bot — the five-minute assembly. RoboTTT is the only method with any full successes on Gear Bot (2/10). Every baseline scores 0/10.

RoboTTT-8K reaches 71.5% average task completion score; the same model pretrained at 1K reaches 43.9% (+63% relative gain); best short-context baseline GR00T N1.7 Hist. reaches 45.6% (RoboTTT-8K is +57% higher).   • One-shot imitation on Circuit. Conditioning on one in-context human video of an unseen configuration, using the identical “assemble circuit” prompt across configs: RoboTTT succeeds on 6/10 trials with a 65% task completion score; GDN fails entirely (0/10, 33% score), picking up wrong components or assembling in the wrong order.

  • DAgger Distillation on Pup Go Car. From a pool of 100 DAgger trajectories (50 each collected with RoboTTT and GR00T N1.7 as the base policy): standard DAgger fine-tuning on corrections alone yields +9% average across the four methods and +13% across the two sequence models; DAgger Distillation yields +33% average (+36% RoboTTT, +29% GDN).

Ablations

  • Sequence action forcing. Removing it collapses closed-loop performance on Pup Go Car; the resulting inaccurate motions leave the robot unable to make meaningful progress.
  • Fast-model architecture. Replacing the two-layer MLP with a linear layer (TTT Linear) drops performance 27% relative to the MLP fast model; TTT-linear still beats GR00T N1.7 but remains suboptimal.
  • Component roadmap on Pup Go Car. Starting from a TTT MLP over state tokens only: adding action tokens through the TTT layers yields +23% relative improvement (past actions help capture environment dynamics); adding 16 learned register tokens yields another +18% relative improvement. Register tokens do not help GR00T N1.7 by themselves, so the isolation shows they only pay off when paired with TTT’s temporal modeling.

Limitations

  • Below 1K pretraining context, RoboTTT still outperforms GDN at matched context length but falls short of longer-context variants; the paper attributes this to rollout horizons exceeding the training context (1K timesteps ~30 seconds — shorter than the shortest task episode), so at inference the fast weights update far beyond the window seen in training and positional embeddings extend to unseen positions.
  • All experiments use a single YAM bimanual setup with four RGB cameras; cross-embodiment scaling is left to future work.

MeanFlowNFT: Bringing Forward-Process RL to Average-Velocity Generators

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-07-16 Flow matching · RL · Few-step generation · T2I · T2V

Overview

  • Authors: Yushi Huang, Xiangxin Zhou, Jun Zhang, Liefeng Bo, Tianyu Pang (corresponding). Affiliations: Tencent Hunyuan (primary) and HKUST. Public deliverables: project page, GitHub, Hugging Face weights.
  • Setting. Flow-matching / diffusion models synthesize a sample by integrating an instantaneous velocity field over many sequential steps, which is slow. MeanFlow (Geng et al. 2026) removes this by predicting the average velocity over a time interval, so a few steps suffice at inference. Reinforcement learning — typically GRPO-style policy gradients over the reverse process (Flow-GRPO, Xue et al.) — is the standard tool for aligning these generators to reward. DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al. 2026) instead RL-tunes directly on the forward process: it contrasts positive/negative generations split by reward to form an implicit policy-improvement direction and folds rewards into a flow-matching objective — likelihood-free and solver-agnostic.
  • Gap the paper closes: DiffusionNFT optimizes the instantaneous velocity v(x_t, t), but MeanFlow samples with an average velocity u(x_t, s, t) over an interval [s, t]. Applying DiffusionNFT directly to a MeanFlow network is ill-defined and, as the appendix shows, breaks the policy-improvement guarantee.
  • Central claim: MeanFlowNFT is the first forward-process RL framework for MeanFlow generators. It applies the DiffusionNFT objective to an induced instantaneous-velocity predictor derived from the average-velocity network via the MeanFlow identity, keeping training likelihood-free and leaving the efficient few-step sampler unchanged.

Methodology

  • MeanFlow identity as the bridge. The MeanFlow average velocity satisfies u(x_t, s, t) = (1/(t-s)) ∫_s^t v(x_τ, τ) dτ and reduces to v(x_t, t) as s → t. Differentiating the displacement form (t-s)u = ∫_s^t v dτ with respect to t yields the exact identity v(x_t, t) = u(x_t, s, t) + (t-s)⋅(d/dt)u(x_t, s, t), so a MeanFlow network u_θ yields an induced instantaneous-velocity predictor v̂_θ := u_θ + (t-s)⋅(du_θ/dt).
  • MeanFlowNFT objective (central named contribution). Problem. DiffusionNFT’s objective is defined for instantaneous velocity v. Applying it naively to a MeanFlow network optimizes the wrong quantity and, as Appendix B proves, fails to guarantee policy improvement. Mechanism. Apply the DiffusionNFT-style objective to the induced instantaneous predictor v̂_θ instead of the average-velocity network directly. Training is on the forward noising process, so no reverse-trajectory sampling and no likelihood estimation are needed; the trainable and reference predictors in the algorithm share the same finite-difference estimate of the total-derivative term, computed along the forward-process conditional velocity. Sampling remains an average-velocity few-step MeanFlow rollout. Why it works. The paper proves that in an idealized setting, the optimum of the induced predictor matches DiffusionNFT’s improved-policy target and this improvement provably carries over to the average-velocity generator. Beats naive drop-in adaptations of DiffusionNFT to MeanFlow (which the appendix shows break the guarantee) and beats reverse-process RL (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) because it never has to sample reverse trajectories or estimate per-step likelihoods.
  • Practical implementation. The total-derivative term du/dt in the induced predictor is approximated by a finite difference. The trainable and reference networks share the same finite-difference estimate at each training step, which the appendix shows preserves DiffusionNFT’s policy-improvement direction in the average-velocity network.

Evaluation & Results

  • Text-to-video (Wan2.1 1.3B). 4-step MeanFlowNFT reaches VBench score 84.33, surpassing 50-step LongCat-Video RL at 82.57. This is roughly a 12× sampling-step reduction at higher aggregate VBench.
  • Text-to-image (SD3.5-M). MeanFlowNFT outperforms prior SOTA RL-tuned few-step generators on 6 of 8 reported metrics; qualitative comparisons vs Flow-GRPO (40 steps), DiffusionNFT (40 steps), and AnyFlow (4 steps) are in Figure 2.
  • The paper reports MeanFlowNFT can even surpass multi-step RL-tuned diffusion baselines using only a few sampling steps, and that it scales gracefully at test time (more sampling steps improve quality further, with full test-time-scaling curves in Appendix E).

Ablations

  • The paper trades off induced-predictor design choices (linear vs. exact total-derivative, shared vs. separate finite-difference estimates for trainable and reference); full ablation numbers are in Appendix D.
  • Appendix B analyzes why naive plug-ins — applying DiffusionNFT directly to u_θ without going through the MeanFlow identity — lose DiffusionNFT’s strict policy-improvement guarantee.

Other

  • Open weights on Hugging Face (Harahan/MeanFlowNFT) and open source on GitHub, so the method is directly plug-in for public MeanFlow generators.
  • Positioning vs. AnyFlow (Alibaba, May 14 review): AnyFlow distills a many-step video diffusion model into a few-step generator via on-policy flow-map distillation; MeanFlowNFT instead does RL-alignment of an already-few-step MeanFlow generator on the forward process. The two are complementary rather than substitutable — AnyFlow reduces sampling steps, MeanFlowNFT aligns the few-step generator to reward.
