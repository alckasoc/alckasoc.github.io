From Synthesis to Removal: Physics-Grounded Reflection Simulation and Diffusion-Based Video Dereflection

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv · HF Daily Papers 2026-08-12 Video reflection removal · Diffusion prior adaptation · Physics-grounded synthesis · Benchmark release

Overview

  • Authors: Zepeng Wang*, Jiagao Hu*, Fuhao Li*, Yuxuan Chen, Fei Wang, Daiguo Zhou (equal contribution starred). All at MiLM Plus, Xiaomi Inc. Paper flagged for AAAI 2027 submission per the project page keywords. arXiv 2608.11562; project page and figures at codingwzp.github.io/VideoDereflection_S2R; code and weights promised (“coming soon”) but not yet released.
  • Setup: single unified framework S2R for video reflection removal, with three named contributions — S2R-Synthesis (data generator), S2R-Removal (removal model), and S2R-Benchmark (evaluation split: 60 paired videos + 50 real-world videos). Each of the three gets a mechanism explanation below.
  • Positioning: video reflection removal has lagged the single-image variant because paired video ground truth is essentially unobtainable at scale (you would need a rig that captures the same scene twice with and without a pane of glass in perfect camera-motion sync). Prior video methods (DSRNet, DSIT, RDNet, DAI) either applied image models frame-by-frame or trained on very small paired video sets; the strongest recent generative baseline GenSIRR (CVPR’26) is single-image and, when applied to video, runs at ~7s per frame. S2R’s claim is that a physics-grounded synthetic paired-video pipeline plus a two-stage adaptation of a pretrained video diffusion prior beats all of those on both video and image benchmarks, at a per-frame cost lower even than the non-diffusion RDNet.

Methodology

  • The full framework closes a loop: S2R-Synthesis produces paired (reflected, clean) videos with controllable glass properties, S2R-Removal is trained on those pairs to recover the transmission video, and S2R-Benchmark scores the removal on both paired and real videos. The three contributions each address a distinct bottleneck.
  • Named contribution — S2R-Synthesis (structure/appearance decoupling + Physics-Grounded Augmentation).
    • Problem. Training a video reflection-removal model needs paired (reflected, clean) videos. Real paired video is essentially uncollectable at scale, and traditional pixel-space synthesis (blend a “reflection layer” RGB video with a “transmission layer” RGB video using an alpha or additive rule) does not reproduce roughness-induced blur, thickness-induced ghosting, or reflectance variation — three physically distinct effects a real pane of glass produces. UE5-based simulation reproduces the physics but the rendered appearance domain-gaps against real footage.
    • Mechanism. Two-stage pipeline that decouples reflection structure from reflection appearance. Stage A trains a structure-guided video renderer to map a structure signal (an intermediate representation of what the reflection geometrically is) to realistic reflected-video appearance, using FLUX-generated pseudo reflection videos as its training pairs. Stage B takes two independent clean videos as the transmission and reflection source, fuses their structure-space conditions through the Physics-Grounded Augmentation module (PGA — the paper’s operator set is P/St/Pa/Re/Th/Ro for Planar/Static/Partial/Reflectance/Thickness/Roughness controls), and then invokes the Stage-A renderer to produce a realistic reflected video paired with the original transmission video. This gives arbitrary paired data whose glass properties are directly controllable rather than baked into the training set.
    • Why. The structure/appearance decoupling is what makes physics controllable without giving up appearance realism: PGA acts in the structure space (where roughness, thickness, and reflectance have clean parametrizations) while the renderer supplies photorealism it learned from FLUX-generated pseudo data. Ablation Table 1 shows this decomposition is what pays. Panel A (data source) reports RDNet trained on the OpenRR-1k train split reaches 28.05 PSNR / 0.9465 SSIM on the OpenRR-1k test split; adding traditional synthesis brings it to 31.98/0.9619 (+3.93 PSNR); adding S2R physics-only augmentation instead brings it to 32.35/0.9643; combining both reaches 33.26/0.9679 (+5.21 PSNR over the base). Panel B (PGA variants) shows the six PGA operators are additive: starting from P alone at 32.35, incrementally enabling St, Pa, Re, Th, Ro lifts PSNR to 33.70 and Full PGA to 34.13 (+1.78 over P alone). No single operator carries the improvement; the operator set is what matters, which is the expected signature of a physics-grounded rather than ad-hoc augmentation.
  • Named contribution — S2R-Removal (two-stage adaptation of a video diffusion prior).
    • Problem. A pretrained video diffusion prior encodes strong temporal appearance statistics but has no awareness of the reflection/transmission decomposition. Naive fine-tuning of such a prior on paired dereflection data (a) burns the temporal prior, and (b) tends to keep sub-visible reflection residuals in the latent that a decoder cannot suppress. Meanwhile, standard multi-step denoising during inference costs multiple seconds per frame, which is what makes GenSIRR unusable for video despite its removal quality.
    • Mechanism. Two stages. Stage I — reflection-aware latent adaptation. The video diffusion prior is fine-tuned so that its latent representation is explicitly reflection-aware, supervised by the standard diffusion loss ℒdiff plus a ℒint residual-derived intensity loss (loss on the reflected-vs-clean intensity residual, which the paper uses as a proxy for “where in the latent the reflection lives”). Stage II — one-step pixel-geometric refinement. A single denoising step is applied to produce the transmission video, supervised by a reconstruction loss ℒrec, a structural loss ℒssim, and a depth-consistency loss ℒdepth. The whole inference path is one denoising step, which is where the 87.09 ms/frame budget comes from.
    • Why. Splitting adaptation into “make the latent know about reflection” and “refine in pixel/geometric space” lets each stage keep the losses it needs without corrupting the other: Stage I’s intensity loss teaches the latent to represent the reflection residual, and Stage II’s SSIM + depth losses recover clean transmission structure without back-propagating pixel losses through the whole diffusion trajectory. The one-step Stage-II refinement is the mechanism that makes the model 83× faster than GenSIRR (7215 → 87 ms/frame at 832×480) while improving on GenSIRR’s PSNR by 1.80 on S2R-Ref. Ablation Table 2 stacks the losses on the S2R-Ref split: ℒdiff only gives 27.00 / 0.8348; adding ℒint gives 27.71 / 0.8430; adding ℒrec gives 28.21 / 0.8513; adding ℒssim gives 28.55 / 0.8566; adding ℒdepth gives 28.84 / 0.8594. Depth consistency is what closes the gap; without it the Stage-II refinement rounds off transmission geometry in ways that show up on SSIM.
  • Named contribution — S2R-Benchmark (S2R-Ref + S2R-Real).
    • Problem. No dedicated video reflection-removal benchmark existed. Prior work reused image-only splits (DSRNet’s Real-20, SIR2, Nature-20) which cannot measure temporal consistency, and it evaluated video methods by qualitative side-by-side. This left removal-vs-preservation trade-offs and temporal flicker unquantified.
    • Mechanism. Two splits released together. S2R-Ref is 60 paired videos captured (or rendered) so that reflected and clean sequences share identical virtual camera motion, enabling full-reference PSNR/SSIM and temporal-consistency (TC) evaluation. S2R-Real is 50 in-the-wild reflection videos spanning multiple glass materials, lighting conditions, reflection strengths, and camera motions, scored by human raters on three axes: reflection Removal quality, transmission Preservation, and TC.
    • Why. Splitting evaluation into paired (numerical, reproducible) and real-world (perceptual, but scoring the axes users actually care about) forces methods to trade off the axes explicitly rather than optimizing PSNR at the cost of oversuppressing transmission or introducing flicker. The Preservation axis is the sharpest signal here: S2R-Real Preservation lifts to 0.980 vs the next-best DAI at 0.871 and GenSIRR at 0.831, meaning the model does not carve out transmission content when removing reflection, which is the classic failure mode of aggressive dereflection.

Ablations

  • Synthesis data source (Table 1, Panel A). Base RDNet (OpenRR-1k train) on OpenRR-1k test: 28.05 / 0.9465. + Traditional pixel-space synthesis: 31.98 / 0.9619 (+3.93). + S2R physics-only synthesis: 32.35 / 0.9643 (+4.30). + Both: 33.26 / 0.9679 (+5.21). Combining traditional and physics-grounded synthesis dominates either alone.
  • PGA operator composition (Table 1, Panel B). P alone: 32.35. P + St: 32.81. P + St + Pa: 33.62. P + St + Pa + Re: 33.58. P + St + Pa + Re + Th: 33.70. Full PGA (adds Ro): 34.13. The reflectance operator is the one non-monotonic step (PSNR ticks down 0.04 but SSIM ticks up), and the full operator set is +1.78 PSNR over P alone. This is the additive signature the physics-grounded framing predicts.
  • S2R-Removal loss stack (Table 2). Progressive enablement on S2R-Ref: ℒdiff: 27.00 / 0.8348. + ℒint: 27.71 / 0.8430. + ℒrec: 28.21 / 0.8513. + ℒssim: 28.55 / 0.8566. + ℒdepth: 28.84 / 0.8594. Every loss adds; the intensity loss (Stage I) and the depth loss (Stage II) are the two largest single contributors.
  • Stage I vs Stage II (Figure 10). Qualitative comparison isolates what Stage II adds on top of Stage I — the paper reports Stage II removes fine-grained reflection residuals and recovers transmission texture that Stage I leaves smoothed out. This is consistent with the quantitative Stage-II-only lift of +1.13 PSNR / +0.0164 SSIM in Table 2 (row 2 → row 5).
  • Comparison with UE5-based synthesis (Figure 3). Under controlled variation of roughness, reflectance, and thickness, S2R-Synthesis reproduces the physics trends more faithfully than a UE5-based reflection synthesis pipeline. The paper does not report a quantitative PSNR/SSIM on this comparison; the claim is qualitative and rests on the reader inspecting Figure 3.

Other

  • Limitations, per the paper. S2R-Removal inherits the base video diffusion prior’s failure modes on lighting extremes and rare glass geometries; when the reflection component overlaps transmission content at similar intensity, Stage II can leave residual ambiguity. The paper does not evaluate on very-long-form video (multi-minute clips) or on live-camera streaming.
  • Reproducibility. Code and pretrained weights are listed as “coming soon” on the project page as of publication. The S2R-Ref and S2R-Real splits are the benchmark contribution and are expected to accompany the release. Nothing is currently on Hugging Face for direct use; the paper submitter (HigherHu) confirmed release intent in the HF Papers thread.
