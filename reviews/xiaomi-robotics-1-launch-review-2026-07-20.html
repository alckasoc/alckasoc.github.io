TL;DR

Focus

Xiaomi Robotics ships the Tier 1 launch of the window: Xiaomi-Robotics-1, a foundational vision-language-action (VLA) model at three scales (2.6B, 5.1B, 10.5B) pretrained on over 100,000 hours of real-world UMI-collected manipulation trajectories under a two-stage recipe (embodiment-free pre-training on state-transition-captioned UMI data, then cross-embodiment post-training on ~10K hours of robot data). The model couples a Qwen3-VL backbone to a matched-depth, half-hidden-size DiT action head via a Mixture-of-Transformers arrangement, adds a Choice-Policies auxiliary VLM regression head for faster convergence, and generates H-step action chunks by flow-matching Euler integration. It sets a new state-of-the-art of 57.4% on RoboCasa365 (previous best: ABot-M0.6 at 46.6%), 20.07 avg on RoboDojo (vs the prior best 13.07), 74.5% on RoboCasa, and 59.1% on VLABench. Downstream, it reaches 75% average success (90% progress) on four novel dexterous / mobile tasks with less than 10 hours of demonstrations per task — almost double π0.5’s 40% under the same budget — and scales further to 85% with <40 hours per task. The companion Tier 3 story is Alibaba Qwen’s Qwen3.8-Max preview, previewed at WAIC 2026 as a 2.4T-parameter multimodal model “second only to Fable 5” but shipped without a benchmark table, active-parameter count, model card, or license.

Competitiveness

Xiaomi-Robotics-1 is the strongest published open robot foundation policy on the current mainline VLA benchmark battery. Its +10.8-point RoboCasa365 lead over ABot-M0.6 (Alibaba AMAP, July 15 review) is the largest single-launch jump on that benchmark in 2026, and its 7.0-point RoboDojo gap over the prior best is even larger relative to the scale. Against π0.5 — the reigning US frontier VLA reference — the four-task low-data ablation is decisive: 75% vs 40% average success on the same held-out phone packing / laundry loading / printer refilling / box packing suite at <10 hours/task. The result puts Xiaomi Robotics at parity or ahead of every other 2026 Chinese-lab embodied foundation launch this quarter (Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 world model, ABot-N1, ABot-AgentOS), and the paper’s clean data- and model-scaling curves (out-of-the-box real-robot success rising monotonically from 26% without action pre-training to 75% at 100% data, and from 61% at 2B to 79% at 10B) are the first clean published scaling law on real-robot success from a single VLA family. Xiaomi-Robotics-1: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models with over 100K Hours of Real-World Trajectories

Tier 1 · Technical Report arXiv 2026-07-16 Robot foundation models · VLA · Flow matching · UMI · Scaling

Overview

  • Authors: Xiaomi Robotics Team (Jun Guo, Piaopiao Jin, Jason Li, Peiyan Li, Yingyan Li, Futeng Liu, Wanli Peng, Optimus Qin, Yifei Su, Nan Sun, Qiao Sun, Runze Suo, Heyun Wang, Yunhong Wang, Rujie Wu, Caoyu Xia, Lina Zhang, Jack Zhao, Guoliang Chen, Wenlong Chen, Xinze He, +12 more). Affiliation: Xiaomi Robotics. Correspondence: mi-robotics@xiaomi.com. Project page: robotics.xiaomi.com/xiaomi-robotics-1.html; code + GitHub to be released.
  • Central claim: robotics can follow the LLM/VLM scaling recipe if it treats a large, embodiment-free real-world manipulation corpus (over 100,000 hours of UMI handheld-gripper trajectories) as the pretraining substrate, and reserves cross-embodiment robot data for post-training alignment. The paper backs this with three separate scaling curves: pretraining validation loss vs data scale, pretraining validation loss vs model size, and out-of-the-box real-robot success rate vs each of the two.
  • This is a distinct model line from Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 (July 15 review), which is a 38B multimodal autoregressive world foundation model initialized from EMU3.5. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 is a smaller (2.6B–10.5B) end-to-end VLA policy built on Qwen3-VL + DiT. The two share the Xiaomi Robotics team, but no weights.
  • Reported flagship results: 57.4% avg on RoboCasa365 (previous SOTA 46.6%), 20.07 avg on RoboDojo (previous SOTA 13.07), 74.5% on RoboCasa, 59.1% on VLABench; 75% average success on four novel real-robot tasks in the low-data (<10 hours/task) setting vs 40% for π0.5. A single Xiaomi-Robotics-1-10B policy also completes a room-scale 10-minute mobile suitcase-packing task autonomously.

Architecture

  • Mixture-of-Transformers VLA (central named contribution). Problem. Existing VLAs either bolt a small action head onto a frozen VLM (VLM under-participates in action generation) or share one transformer between the VLM and action head (VLM compute dominates and inference slows). Neither cleanly amortizes the VLM cost across many DiT forward passes. Mechanism. A Mixture-of-Transformers architecture couples a pretrained Qwen3-VL backbone to a diffusion transformer (DiT) with the same number of layers but a smaller hidden size. Per timestep, the VLM encodes (ot, l) once and exposes its KV cache. The DiT conditions on the VLM KV cache, the robot proprioceptive state token st, and a per-timestep flow-matching noise level τ, and generates an H-step action chunk at:t+H by 5-step Euler integration of a learned vector field, with τ injected through adaLN. Why. Halving the DiT hidden size relative to the VLM concentrates capacity in the language-visual reasoning path while making the flow-matching inner loop cheap; the VLM cost is further amortized by sampling 4 flow-matching timesteps per VLM forward pass and packing all resulting DiT inputs into a single DiT pass. Beats single-transformer VLAs on inference speed at the same total parameter budget, and beats frozen-VLM heads by letting the VLM re-shape its own representations toward action generation via the auxiliary Choice-Policies head.
  • Choice-Policies auxiliary action head on the VLM (named contribution). Problem. Adding a VLM-side action head has historically caused shortcut learning — the DiT stops grounding in vision and language and just copies the VLM’s action tokens. Mechanism. The proprioceptive state is MLP-encoded to a token; K action-query tokens and K score-query tokens are appended to the VLM sequence. The VLM predicts K candidate action chunks and K scores; winner-takes-all: only the candidate with the smallest L1 distance to the ground truth contributes to the action loss, while the scores regress against the L1 distances themselves. Crucially, DiT attention is masked to exclude the action-related VLM tokens from the KV cache. Why. The VLM is pushed toward action-supportive representations, but the DiT is denied a copy-and-paste shortcut, so it must recompute the action from grounded visual-linguistic context. Empirically this accelerates convergence without collapsing the DiT.

Pre-training

  • Scalable auto-labeling pipeline (central named contribution). Problem. Manual segmentation and instruction-labeling of 100,000 hours of trajectory data is prohibitive; naive per-trajectory VLM captioning is I/O-bound and would take months. Mechanism. A producer–consumer pipeline decouples clip segmentation (CPU worker threads write fixed-length clips to an in-memory filesystem) from caption labeling (client threads keep hundreds of Qwen3.5-27B captioning requests in flight). The VLM captions each clip as a natural-language description of the state transition of the grippers and interacting objects, not a task instruction. Why. Two weeks of wall time labels the whole 100K-hour corpus, and the state-transition target is a strictly denser training signal than an instruction: the language explicitly specifies what changes and how, so the model is trained to bring the scene from the current state into the described target state. That is a well-defined pointwise supervision even where the same visual clip is compatible with many high-level instructions.
  • Data. Over 100,000 hours of UMI handheld-gripper egocentric trajectories collected across households, commercial premises, industrial sites, offices, and outdoor spaces (1,700+ scenarios). Embodiment-free by design: because UMI grippers are handheld and not tied to a specific robot morphology, the data spans a much broader distribution of objects, environments, and manipulation behaviors than teleoperated robot data can practically reach.
  • Objective. Joint minimization of LFlow (DiT flow-matching loss with τ ~ Beta(1.5, 1)-derived noise levels, weighted to noisier timesteps) + LRegression (VLM Choice-Policies winner-takes-all L1 + score MSE) + λ · LNTP (co-training next-token prediction on a curated VL dataset from Xiaomi-Robotics-0, with λ = 0.1). Data mix: VL : UMI = 1 : 9. Throughput trick: pack all VL tokens in a batch into one VLM sequence; per sample, run 4 flow-matching timesteps sharing that VLM KV cache and pack them into one DiT pass.

Post-training

  • Goal. Bridge two gaps at once: (i) map UMI-gripper action generation onto the action spaces of real robot embodiments, and (ii) shift language conditioning from state-transition descriptions (pretraining) to the imperative instructions humans naturally use with robots.
  • Data mix (~10,000 hours total). 7,200+ hours of in-house real-robot data collected with mobile manipulators and dual-arm robots across real homes; 1,000+ hours of human-annotated UMI data with temporal segments and instruction labels; open-source robot datasets Bridge V2, RT-1, DROID. Idle segments are filtered out. Trajectory sampling ratio VL : open-source robot : instruction-UMI : in-house robot = 0.5 : 0.5 : 0.5 : 8.5.
  • Unified cross-embodiment action space. Arm actions use relative delta end-effector poses in the current EE frame; EE-frame orientations are unified across UMI and all robot data so that similar motions yield consistent target values regardless of hardware. For mobile robots, base action is base velocity and waist action is relative delta waist position. Heterogeneous embodiments share one unified action vector with missing dimensions masked out during loss computation.
  • Objective. Same three-term loss as pretraining; same asynchronous inference recipe (inherited from Xiaomi-Robotics-0) for downstream fine-tuning.

Evaluation & Results

  • Pre-training scaling. Data scaling (Xiaomi-Robotics-1-5B, 12.5% / 25% / 50% / 100% of a 20k-hour subset): validation action MSE decreases monotonically with data scale; the 12.5% and 25% runs overfit (loss re-increases), while 50% and 100% keep dropping, with the 100% run showing a steeper descent. Model scaling (2B, 5B, 10B on the same 20k-hour data): action MSE decreases monotonically with model size, but the gap is smaller than the gap across data scales — suggesting data volume is currently the tighter bottleneck at this parameter count.
  • Post-training out-of-the-box real-robot success (unseen envs and object instances). Four tasks: shoe storage, bag packing, table organization, sofa tidying.
    • Scaling pretraining data (5B, 4 pretraining scales + no-action-pretraining baseline): overall success rises monotonically 26% → 53% → ... → 75% at 100% pretraining data. Shoe tidying goes from 0% without action pretraining to 75% with 100% data. Even 12.5% pretraining more than doubles the baseline (26% → 53%). No saturation: 50% → 100% still adds +6 points.
    • Scaling model size (2B / 5B / 10B all pretrained on the 20k-hour subset): overall success rises 61% → 75% → 79%. Shoe tidying rises 58% → 75% → 92% across the three sizes.
  • Downstream fine-tuning (4 held-out challenging tasks: phone packing, laundry loading, printer refilling, box packing).
    • Low-data setting (~36 h total, avg <10 h/task): Xiaomi-Robotics-1 reaches 75% average success and 90% average progress, vs π0.5 40%/66%.
    • High-data setting (144 h total): reaches 85% average success. Every task benefits from more data; Xiaomi-Robotics-1 is substantially more data-efficient than π0.5 and Xiaomi-Robotics-0 in both settings.
    • Task-level highlights (low-data): printer refilling (deformable-object manipulation) goes from best-baseline 20% to Xiaomi-Robotics-1 70%; laundry loading (long-horizon mobile) reaches 80% success / 96% progress where π0.5 fails to open the washing machine and Xiaomi-Robotics-0 cannot complete the task; box packing hits 100% at high data across all methods.

Ablations

  • Action pre-training is decisive. Skipping the UMI pre-training stage entirely (initialize post-training from stock Qwen3-VL) collapses out-of-the-box success from 75% to 26%. Contact-rich tasks (shoe tidying: 0% without pre-training vs 75% with) show the largest gain.
  • Data volume beats model capacity at billions-parameter scale. Pretraining MSE gaps across data scales exceed gaps across model sizes on the same 20k-hour data. The paper reads this as: current data volume is the primary bottleneck at 2B–10B; further scale should prioritize data.
  • State-transition captions beat instruction captions in pretraining. Post-training uses instruction language; pretraining uses state-transition captions. The paper argues (and Figure 12 in the appendix illustrates) that state-transition captions are strictly denser signals for action learning — a given clip is compatible with many instructions but with essentially one state transition.
  • Action-token attention masking on the DiT side is what makes Choice Policies safe. Without excluding the VLM’s action-related tokens from the DiT’s KV cache, the DiT copy-pastes the VLM action tokens instead of grounding in visual/language context (paper section 2.1).

Qwen3.8-Max-Preview: 2.4T-parameter multimodal preview at WAIC 2026

Tier 3 · Blog Post Alibaba Qwen (X post + Token Plan) 2026-07-19 Flagship model · MoE · Multimodal · Preview
  • What was announced. On July 19 at the World AI Conference in Shanghai, Alibaba’s Qwen team previewed Qwen3.8-Max: a 2.4-trillion-parameter model the team describes as their first multimodal flagship above 1T parameters (text, images, video, documents). The team’s stated positioning is “second only to Fable 5” among the systems it benchmarked. Qwen developer Shuai Bai adds that it should beat Qwen3.7-Max on coding, full-stack development, data analysis, and office workflows.
  • Named contribution — sparse MoE flagship. Problem. The obvious way to keep scaling Qwen’s Max tier past Qwen3.7-Max is to widen the total parameter budget, but a dense scale-up is intractable at frontier serving cost. Mechanism. Consistent with the Qwen3 Max tier lineage (Qwen3-235B-A22B activates 22B/235B; Qwen3-30B-A3B activates ~3B/30B), Qwen3.8-Max is sparse MoE and multimodal, but Alibaba has not disclosed the activated-parameter count. Why (as claimed). Sparse MoE at 2.4T lets Alibaba announce a leaderboard-competitive total parameter count while preserving low activated compute per token; without the activated-parameter number, the serving-cost claim cannot yet be verified.
  • What is confirmed. The preview is live for paying users via Alibaba’s Token Plan, Qoder, and QoderWork at 10% of standard pricing. API surface is OpenAI- and Anthropic-protocol compatible so existing coding-agent harnesses point at it unchanged. Open weights are promised “soon.”
  • What is not yet confirmed. No benchmark table; no active-parameter count; no model card or system card; no Hugging Face repository; no license (Alibaba’s last two Max flagships shipped closed-weight, so the open-weight promise breaks that pattern); no independent-eval numbers on MMLU-Pro, SWE-Bench Verified, GPQA Diamond, Terminal-Bench, or Artificial Analysis / LMArena. The “second only to Fable 5” framing rests on internal evaluation.
  • Context. Timing puts the preview two days after Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3 (2.8T-parameter open-weight, July 17) and on the same day as DeepSeek-V4 general availability at WAIC. As a preview announcement without a technical report or model card, this entry is Tier 3 today; when Alibaba publishes an activated-parameter count, a benchmark table, or a technical report, the launch becomes Tier 1 and moves into that review’s window.
