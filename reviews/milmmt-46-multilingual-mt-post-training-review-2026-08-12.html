Competitiveness

MiLMMT-46 is the first open translation model family reported to hold reference-free-quality parity with Gemini 3 Pro and GPT-5 on 46-language coverage, and the 12B checkpoint keeps that claim at a size that runs on a single H100 — a shift in the open-vs-proprietary MT frontier that previously belonged to Google Translate’s hosted stack and to Gemini-3 and GPT-5 general-purpose systems. The comparison field also includes recent open MT specialists (Seed-X from ByteDance, HY-MT2 from Tencent Hunyuan, TranslateGemma), so the claim is not just “better than the previous open MT baseline” but “better than the current open MT frontier and competitive with the current proprietary frontier.” The paper’s ablation result that on-policy distillation (OPD) matches but does not surpass RL-plus-checkpoint-interpolation is a useful negative result for labs comparing OPD-vs-GRPO+CI recipes at the 12B scale on multilingual generative-quality objectives; recent inkling-family and DAPD work has been converging on OPD as a default, and this data point argues for RL+CI on 46-language MT specifically.

Overview

  • Authors: Chris Han, Pengzhi Gao, Pei Fu, Jian Luan — all Xiaomi Research. Submitted by Pengzhi Gao. Code and weights at github.com/xiaomi-research/gemmax.
  • Setup: MiLMMT-46 is a family of Gemma3-based open multilingual translation models covering 46 languages, released at 1B, 4B, and 12B parameter scales. The v0.1 checkpoints are SFT-only; the v1.0 checkpoints released here add a reference-free RL post-training stage plus a checkpoint-interpolation step with the SFT baseline.
  • Named contributions receiving mechanism explanations below: reference-free GRPO with LID-gated averaged-QE reward and SFT↔RL linear checkpoint interpolation. The paper also reports a negative result for a third alternative — on-policy distillation — that reaches, but does not surpass, the RL+CI frontier.
  • Positioning: the paper’s central claim is that a small open MT family, post-trained with a reference-free RL signal and released under an open license, can hold reference-free-quality parity with proprietary frontier systems (Google Translate, Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5) across 46 languages. If replicable, that is a shift in where the open/proprietary line sits for translation.

Methodology

  • Base checkpoints. Start from MiLMMT-46-v0.1, the supervised-fine-tuned Gemma3-based multilingual translation models at 1B, 4B, and 12B. The upstream Gemma3 base and the SFT stage on the MiLMMT-46 mixture are treated as fixed for the purposes of this paper.
  • Named contribution — reference-free GRPO with LID-gated averaged-QE reward.
    • Problem. Post-training rewards for MT have historically come from reference-based metrics (BLEU / COMET-QE-with-reference) that require aligned bitext and cannot rate the tail of low-resource language pairs where high-quality references do not exist. Reference-free QE models can score any (source, hypothesis) pair, but taking a single QE model as the reward exposes RL to that model’s specific biases (length preferences, script preferences, ID confusion between closely-related languages). Naive GRPO with a single reference-free reward degenerates on the low-resource tail by producing high-QE-score outputs in the wrong target language.
    • Mechanism. The reward is the mean of two independent reference-free QE models, gated by a language-identification classifier. For each sampled translation: (i) run LID on the hypothesis; if the detected language does not match the requested target language, the reward is set to a fixed low value; (ii) if it matches, the reward is the average of the two QE models’ scores. GRPO then uses this scalar as its group-relative advantage signal in the standard way.
    • Why. Averaging two QE models de-biases the reward from any single model’s systematic preferences (a common source of RL reward hacking). Gating by LID directly forbids the “output high-quality text in the wrong language” failure mode that a QE-only reward permits — particularly acute on 46-language RL where the model can drift into pivot languages such as English or Chinese to farm high QE scores. Together these two changes are what makes reference-free RL usable at 46-language coverage without collapsing on the long tail.
  • Named contribution — SFT↔RL linear checkpoint interpolation.
    • Problem. RL post-training on a single reward improves what the reward measures but can degrade other quality axes (fluency on high-resource pairs, register preservation, refusal to hallucinate on rare pairs) that the SFT stage learned but the reward does not cover. Selecting a single RL checkpoint forces one such trade-off.
    • Mechanism. Take the SFT-only checkpoint (v0.1) and the post-RL checkpoint and linearly interpolate their weights in parameter space at a chosen mixing coefficient, producing the released v1.0 checkpoint. Interpolation is applied uniformly across all parameters.
    • Why. Weight-space interpolation recovers SFT-side quality on axes the RL reward did not cover while retaining the RL-side improvement on axes the reward did cover — the parameter-space midpoint dominates either endpoint on the aggregate reference-free score across 46 languages. On-policy distillation, an alternative post-training path the paper also investigates, matches but does not exceed this RL+CI frontier, making RL+CI the empirically preferred recipe at the 12B scale on multilingual MT quality.

Evaluation and results

  • Across 46 languages, MiLMMT-46-v1.0 consistently improves reference-free translation quality over the SFT-only v0.1 baseline at every model scale (1B, 4B, 12B).
  • Beats strong recent open MT baselines: Seed-X (ByteDance), HY-MT2 (Tencent Hunyuan), and TranslateGemma.
  • Achieves leading reference-free scores against evaluated proprietary systems: Google Translate, Gemini 3 Pro, and GPT-5.
  • Released model checkpoints on Hugging Face: MiLMMT-46-1B-v1.0, MiLMMT-46-12B-v1.0, with the 4B checkpoint in the same collection.
  • Live demo hosted at xiaomi-research/milmmt-46-translation; additional Space mirrors at hugging-apps/xiaomi-research-milmmt-46-1b-v1-0 and hugging-apps/xiaomi-research-milmmt-46-4b-v1-0.

Ablations

  • On-policy distillation vs RL+CI. The paper investigates on-policy distillation (OPD) as an alternative to the RL-plus-checkpoint-interpolation post-training pipeline. The empirical finding is that OPD reaches the RL+CI reference-free-quality frontier but does not surpass it — a useful negative result for teams comparing the two post-training pipelines at the 12B scale on multilingual generative-quality objectives, particularly given the recent inkling-family and DAPD papers converging on OPD as a general-purpose default.
