Alibaba (DAMO Academy)

RynnValue: Scaling Robotic Value Foundation Models with Temporal Distance

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv:2608.09853 2026-08-10 Robotics · Reward modeling · Value learning · VLA · Manipulation

Overview

  • Open-source value foundation model for robotic manipulation from Alibaba DAMO Academy, positioned as a drop-in reward interface for downstream generalist policies (VLA fine-tuning, RL-based imitation) rather than as an end-to-end control model in the Rynnbrain / Xiaomi-Robotics mold.
  • Sole named contribution is the training target itself: temporal distance, i.e. the directed cost-to-go from an observation frame to the language-specified goal frame, measured in units of the demonstration's own timeline. Because the label is a timestamp difference, the training corpus can be assembled from any embodiment and any camera view without preference annotators or per-task progress rubrics.
  • Scale reported: 7,000+ hours of manipulation video across heterogeneous embodiments, roughly ~3M instruction-conditioned clips, all trained without any preference-label pass.
  • Headline number: 0.675 average Kendall's τa on RBM-EVAL-OOD, beating the previous fully preference-supervised SOTA at 0.655 and a progress-only counterpart at 0.292. Zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks, embodiments, and viewpoints. Downstream, potential-based reward shaping using RynnValue lifts real-world policy success from 52.5% → 72.5% online and 63.8% → 82.5% offline.

Methodology

Mechanism — Temporal distance as the supervision target

  • Problem. Robot-scale reward learning has two dominant supervision sources and both break under heterogeneity. Preference comparisons require labeled pairs from a matched embodiment / camera / task, do not compose across corpora, and get expensive fast. Normalized progress (t / T) is cheap but only well-defined per demonstration and per task; the same physical state gets a different label depending on which trajectory it is embedded in, and any regression or recovery within a demonstration becomes a "backwards in progress" instance that a monotonic normalized-progress model refuses to represent.
  • Mechanism. RynnValue predicts temporal distance: the number of steps from the current observation to the goal frame that satisfies the language instruction, taken directly from the demonstration's timeline. This label is (a) derivable from raw timestamps — no annotator loop, (b) directional and additive along a trajectory, and (c) sign-consistent across embodiments because the meaning of "how many steps until you reach the goal" transfers even when the embodiment does not. Three architectural / sampling ingredients keep the target learnable at scale:
    • Random temporal sampling. Frames within a clip are sampled non-uniformly rather than at fixed strides, so the model cannot learn to read the clip index as a proxy for the label.
    • Temporal-order shuffling. Some training clips have their frame order perturbed. The model has to recover the goal-relative distance from visual content, not from position in the input sequence — forcing the representation to be genuinely observation-conditioned.
    • Value-isolation attention. A masking pattern that prevents the value head from attending across frames in ways that would let it back-compute distance from adjacent-frame contrast rather than from the goal-conditioned semantics. The paper reports this suppresses shortcut solutions that leave predictions insensitive to failures and regressions.
  • Why it works / why it beats prior. Against preference-based RMs, temporal distance replaces a scarce annotated signal with a signal already implicit in any recorded demonstration, so the training corpus grows with data collection rather than with labeling budget — and evaluation shows the annotation-free supervision outperforms the preference-labeled prior state on the same OOD benchmark (0.675 vs 0.655). Against progress-based RMs, cost-to-go is defined per (observation, goal) rather than per (frame, demonstration), so it degrades gracefully across embodiments and can penalize regression — empirically shown by the 2.3× gap to the progress-only baseline (0.675 vs 0.292). The value-isolation-attention + shuffling suppress the shortcut solutions that would otherwise let a large model reach low training loss without actually being sensitive to failures.

ByteDance

SWE-Bench ProMax: Benchmarking Agents on Large-Scale Multilingual Code Refactoring

Tier 2 · Research Paper (Benchmark, COLM 2026) arXiv:2608.09802 2026-08-10 Coding agents · Benchmark · Refactoring · Multilingual · Evaluation

Overview

  • Expert-curated multilingual coding-agent benchmark of 170 instances drawn from real commits across seven languages: Python, Java, TypeScript, Go, C, C++, Rust. Task type is code refactoring — coordinated, behavior-preserving edits across many files — explicitly chosen because it is (a) meaningfully harder than issue-fixing and (b) resistant to the training-data leakage failure modes that have started to saturate SWE-bench Verified.
  • Named contribution is the benchmark itself, plus a multi-stage curation pipeline built to fix three concrete quality problems documented in prior benchmarks:
    • Verbatim gold-patch memorization — frontier models have been observed to reproduce SWE-bench Verified gold patches from training data, invalidating the resolve-rate signal.
    • Overly narrow tests that reject correct solutions.
    • Overly broad tests that check unstated requirements a well-behaved refactor would not satisfy.
  • Scale per instance: mean 11.4 modified files and 261.6 lines of code, substantially exceeding existing benchmarks. This is the "coordinated cross-file edit" regime that most prior benchmarks approximate poorly.
  • Best-model resolve rate under two agent scaffolds: 41.2%. The benchmark is unsaturated and headroom is real.
  • Public artifact: huggingface.co/datasets/swe-bench-promax/SWE-Bench-ProMax.

Methodology

Mechanism — Multi-stage curation targeting the three failure modes of prior benchmarks

  • Problem. A recent audit found nearly 60% of unsolved SWE-bench Verified instances contain flawed tests: some are so narrow they reject any solution that isn't token-identical to the gold patch, others are so broad they check requirements the issue never states. Independently, frontier models can reproduce gold patches verbatim from training data. Together, these mean SWE-bench Verified is measuring a mixture of test-suite noise and training-set overlap rather than the underlying coding-agent capability. Refactoring benchmarks that would sidestep both problems are underserved.
  • Mechanism. ProMax fixes each failure explicitly:
    • Task selection is on real commits with coordinated cross-file edits. Instances with insufficient complexity or limited cross-file scope are filtered out, leaving 170 instances at an average of 11.4 files and 261.6 LOC touched. This forces the benchmark into the regime where retrieval, dependency tracking, and long-horizon planning matter, not single-file bug fixes.
    • Issue descriptions are rewritten from scratch. Rather than reuse the original commit message or issue body (which can leak the patch structure to a model that has seen the repo), ProMax authors write precise, unambiguous specifications describing what to refactor and what invariants to preserve, without prescribing how.
    • Test suites are manually reviewed. Every test in the retained instance is inspected; overly narrow tests that only accept the specific gold patch are removed, and overly broad tests that check unstated requirements are removed. The remaining suite defines behavior preservation without over-specifying implementation.
    • Seven-language coverage. Same curation pipeline applied across Python, Java, TypeScript, Go, C, C++, Rust — ensuring the benchmark rewards genuine multilingual competence rather than Python-specific tooling.
  • Why it works / why it beats prior. The three curation stages each target a specific way a well-scoring model could pass without actually solving the task: rewritten descriptions remove the leakage channel that let frontier models reproduce gold patches; per-instance test review removes the false-negative and false-positive noise the audit found; the file/LOC / cross-file filters push the difficulty above what per-file bug-fix benchmarks measure. Together they produce a benchmark whose ceiling is 41.2% at current frontier strength — roughly half of the current SWE-bench Verified ceiling and, by construction, not attackable by memorization.

Evaluation & results

  • Frontier models evaluated under two agent scaffolds. The best resolve rate is 41.2%. The abstract does not break out per-model or per-language numbers; the full paper does.
  • Interpretation: cross-file behavior-preserving refactoring is a materially harder problem than SWE-bench Verified for the current generation of coding agents, even absent memorization advantages.

Ablations

  • The curation pipeline itself is the ablation frame: the authors report that the three-stage filter (issue rewriting, test cleanup, cross-file / LOC filtering) is what separates ProMax from an uncurated commit-based benchmark. Numbers on how much each stage moves the score are in the full report.

Other

  • Fits directly alongside the day's discourse on Muse Glimmer and Qwen3.6-27B pushing SWE-Bench Verified past 75. If ProMax holds up as a harder, cleaner target, the interesting head-to-head shifts from "who is best on Verified" to "who is best on cross-file refactoring under a leakage-resistant benchmark".
