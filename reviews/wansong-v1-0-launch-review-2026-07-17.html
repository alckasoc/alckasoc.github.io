Focus

WanSong v1.0 Technical Report

Tier 1 · Technical Report arXiv 2026-07-16 Song generation · Diffusion · Dual-stem · Flow matching · RLHF

Overview

  • Authors: Binghui Chen, Pandeng Li, Yu Liu, Jingren Zhou. Affiliation: Wan Team, Alibaba Group (the same team behind the Wan2.1 video foundation model and the Wan-Streamer interactive video line).
  • WanSong v1.0 is a pure diffusion song-generation foundation model with ~25B parameters. Unlike prior autoregressive (Suno V5, Mureka, Yue, LeVo, SongGen) and cascaded AR→diffusion (DiffRhythm2, SongCreator) pipelines, WanSong treats audio as continuous tokens and generates end-to-end in a single stage, directly emitting dual stems (vocals and BGM as separated channels) for songs up to 5 minutes.
  • The report’s central design bet is that vocal and BGM must be modeled as separate output streams rather than entangled targets: when a single CFG scalar has to trade off vocal accuracy vs. BGM strength, both suffer. Dual-stem output with joint-learning-in-layer resolves the CFG-tradeoff bind.
  • Training data: >6 million hours of multilingual song data, curated through a five-stage pipeline (collection → pre-processing → filtering → captioning → sampling). The VAE alone trains on ~2.6×10^8 audio clips balanced at Music : Speech : Sound = 50% : 40% : 10%.
  • Public deliverable: benchmark plus technical report. The paper does not disclose weights or a checkpoint URL; it is a launch of the WanSong bench and the WanSong v1.0 model card.

Architecture

  • 1-D continuous VAE. Compresses a 44.1 kHz stereo waveform x ∈ R^{2×T} to a latent z ∈ R^{C_z × T′} with C_z = 64 and downsampling factor 1024 (~43.1 Hz latent frame rate). This is half the compression of Stable Audio 2 (2048), and the report frames that halving as the single most impactful architectural decision — validated by ablations below.
  • VAE training objective. Adversarial training with generator loss L_G = λ_mag L_mag + λ_fmap L_fmap + λ_adv L_Adv(G;D) + λ_KL L_KL, where L_mag is a multi-resolution STFT magnitude loss computed on both Mid-Side and Left-Right channels (crucial for stereo fidelity), L_fmap is an ℓ1 discriminator feature-matching loss, L_Adv is a hinge adversarial loss, and L_KL is KL to a standard normal prior. Trained on 32×A100 for 1.5M steps with 1.5s stereo crops; random noise augmentation on latents in the late stage robustifies the decoder against OOD artifacts at inference.
  • Hybrid-MMDit diffusion backbone. Text tokens (from a decoder-only LLM) and dual-stem audio continuous tokens (from the VAE) are concatenated into one sequence and fed into a transformer-based diffusion model. Following MMDit and Flux’s hybrid variant, WanSong does not learn per-modality parameters in every layer — only a subset of layers are “modality-split.” The layer split ratio is set to N/(N+K) = 0.3 (~30% modality-split blocks, ~70% shared). Fully-shared AdaLN is used within each block, following the parameter-efficient recipe from Wan2.1.
  • Tokens from different samples are packed into a single sequence at training time to improve throughput and per-batch token utilization, a standard MMDit-scaling trick.
  • Dual-stem output with joint-learning-in-layer. This is the paper’s central named contribution. Problem. A single CFG scalar cannot preserve both vocal phoneme accuracy and BGM strength — large CFG improves phonemes but suppresses BGM; small CFG restores BGM but degrades vocals. Mechanism. At the model’s output, vocal and BGM tokens are produced as two independent streams (two heads / two channel groups). At the per-block level inside the transformer, they are treated as two channels of the same token, so every block simultaneously learns each stem’s marginal distribution and their cross-dependencies. Loss is a weighted sum of two flow-matching MSEs, one per stem, with coefficients α_vocal and α_bgm. Why it works. The two output streams give CFG one knob per stem (or the flexibility to drop CFG on one branch entirely), while the shared per-block channel packing preserves the vocal-BGM correlations that a naive two-tower dual model would sever. This beats prior source-separation-after-AR-generation because the model never had to entangle them in the first place, and it beats single-stream diffusion (Stable Audio 2, DiffRhythm2) because it exposes stem-level editability for downstream production without a source-separation post-step.

Pre-training

  • Flow-matching objective. Follows Rectified Flows — for latent X_1, noise X_0 ~ N(0, I), and timestep t ∈ [0,1] from a logit-normal schedule, the intermediate latent is X_t = t · X_1 + (1-t) · X_0, and the model predicts the ground-truth velocity V_t = X_1 - X_0. The dual-stem loss is L = α_vocal · E_t[||θ(X_{vocal,t}, C_txt, t) - V_{vocal,t}||^2] + α_bgm · E_t[||θ(X_{bgm,t}, C_txt, t) - V_{bgm,t}||^2] where C_txt is the caption embedding.
  • Three-stage pre-training curriculum. Stage 1 — 90s-duration training on shorter clips to establish local musical structure. Stage 2 — 300s-duration training to scale to full-song time horizons. Stage 3 — supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a curated high-quality subset for commercial-grade finish. The multi-dimensional data labeling (musical style, vocals, instruments, mode/key, language, reverb, emotion, lyrics) is used to schedule the training data flexibly across stages.

Post-training (RLHF)

  • Three reward models trained on human-preference pairs along three axes: musicality, lyric accuracy, and prompt alignment. Each reward model minimizes the Bradley-Terry pairwise loss L = -E_{a+,a-}[log τ(R(a+) - R(a-))].
  • Two-stage RL: DPO then ReFL. The paper frames these as complementary rather than substitutable. DPO uses the pairwise formulation and supervises every timestep t ∈ [0,1], which makes it strong for global-structure optimization but noisy at the fine-detail level. ReFL directly backpropagates from a precise reward evaluation but only operates in the low-noise stage of the diffusion trajectory, which makes it strong at fine details but vulnerable to reward hacking. Running DPO first fixes global structure, then ReFL refines the low-noise regime.

Safety & Limitations

  • The report focuses on capabilities and fidelity; it does not enumerate a safety framework, watermarking, artist-consent policy, or usage restrictions. Given that WanSong ships direct multilingual voice-cloning-adjacent capability (high-fidelity vocal generation controllable by text), the omission is notable for a release in the Suno / Mureka / Udio market segment where those questions are actively litigated.
  • The strongest evaluations (Musicality, Quality 1–5) use WanSong-team-trained evaluators. The Team’s 80k-song musicality evaluator is not open-sourced, and Table 4’s 5.49 vs Suno V5’s 4.18 should be read with that self-eval caveat.
  • No FAD, KLD, or MOS numbers reported. The SongEval metric family and Muq alignment score are used instead, and their scores collapse close together across frontier systems (see Table 3).

SearchOS-V1: Towards Robust Open-Domain Information-Seeking Agent Collaboration

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-07-16 Search agents · Multi-agent · Persistent state · Pipeline scheduling

Overview

  • Authors: Yuyao Zhang, Junjie Gao, Zhengxian Wu, et al. (14 authors), corresponding Ji-Rong Wen and Zhicheng Dou. Affiliation: Ant Group (via the antins-labs/SearchOS release org, mirrored on the Hugging Face Ant Group paper page); co-authors from Renmin University of China’s Gaoling School of AI.
  • SearchOS-V1 targets the dominant failure mode of long-horizon information-seeking agents: as history grows, both single-agent and multi-agent systems lose track of task progress, and when a search sub-goal fails they get trapped in repetitive query loops that burn the search budget without producing new evidence.
  • The framing move is to formalize open-domain information seeking as relational schema completion with grounded citations: agents discover entities, populate attributes across linked tables, and anchor every value to a source. This turns “did we make progress” from a soft judgment into a schema-fill delta.
  • On WideSearch and GISA, SearchOS-V1 leads all metrics among the evaluated single- and multi-agent baselines. Code at antins-labs/SearchOS.

Methodology

  • Search-Oriented Context Management (SOCM). This is the paper’s main named contribution. Problem. In today’s multi-agent search stacks, the shared context is either a raw scratchpad or an implicit chat history — both scale poorly with turn count, and neither exposes “what has already been tried and failed.” That opacity is what drives the repeat-query loops. Mechanism. SOCM externalizes the evolving search state into four typed, persistent objects: a Frontier Task queue (the tasks still to attempt), an Evidence Graph (grounded citations linked to schema fields), a Coverage Map (which schema slots are still unfilled), and a Failure Memory (which query × source combinations have already dead-ended). Sub-agents read from and write to these objects instead of just accumulating tokens in a shared prompt. Why. Explicit persistent state means (i) the scheduler can price freed compute slots against unresolved coverage rather than by round-robin, and (ii) query planners can consult Failure Memory to avoid re-firing a known-bad plan — the exact anti-loop mechanism prior single- and multi-agent baselines lacked. This improves over prior context-management approaches (memory-augmented ReAct, chain-of-agents scratchpads) by moving from a monolithic chat log to a typed relational state that the scheduler and planner can each query independently.
  • Pipeline-parallel scheduling. Built on top of SOCM. Sub-agents execute in overlapped pipeline slots; as one finishes, the freed slot is refilled with a task chosen to target an unresolved Coverage Map gap (not the next task in the queue). This maximizes utilization and throughput and biases compute toward closing the schema, not toward exploring dead ends.
  • Search Tool Middleware Harness. A shim between the agent policy and the actual search tool. It intercepts model↔tool interactions to (i) record grounded evidence into the Evidence Graph, (ii) detect stalls or budget exhaustion and react (kill the sub-agent, spawn a new plan), and (iii) expose a reusable hierarchical skill system with two skill types — strategy skills (how to plan a search) and access skills (how to reach a source) — that persist across runs so a Failure Memory entry from one run informs the next.

Evaluation & Results

  • Evaluated on WideSearch and GISA. On both benchmarks SearchOS-V1 “leads all metrics among the evaluated single- and multi-agent baselines” per the abstract. The paper does not disclose the specific benchmark scores in the arXiv abstract; the full metric table lives in the paper body.
  • Design implication: the persistent state architecture is complementary to the reasoning/tool-use RL work that DeepSeek, OpenAI, and Anthropic have been shipping — SearchOS-V1 attacks the coordination layer while those attack the policy layer. A future frontier stack likely combines both.

Other

  • The paper explicitly targets the failure mode Anthropic has been openly discussing for research-agent deployments and that DeepSeek’s OpenSeeker-v2 also flagged: the compounding cost of search-budget waste in multi-turn autonomous research. SearchOS’s answer — explicit typed state plus a coverage-driven scheduler — is a competing design against the prevalent “bigger memory + smarter policy” approach.
