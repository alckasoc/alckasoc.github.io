EnvHarness: Awakening Static Worlds for Agent Learning

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-20 Agent RL, Environment Generation, Verifier-Preserving Wrappers

Overview

  • Chengsong Huang, Zifeng Wang, Rujun Han et al. (Google DeepMind + collaborators). Positions the problem of RL agent training as one of environment adaptation, not policy-only optimization: hand-built environments are blind to the current policy's failure modes, and stay blind as the policy improves.
  • Two named contributions: EnvHarness, a programmable plug-in layer that wraps a static environment and reshapes its behaviour without touching underlying logic; and EnvRigger, a black-box synthesizer that produces harness components targeting flaws diagnosed from the current policy's execution trajectories, then validates them via fresh rollouts.
  • Reports up to +9.0pp on held-out instances against both original environments and domain-specific environment-generation pipelines, with 9.8% fewer execution steps, across five benchmarks in four domains. Also delivers a superior optimization signal for downstream RL — enabling continuous, targeted co-evolution of policy and environment.

Methodology

  • EnvHarness — the mechanism.
    • Problem. Prior environment-generation pipelines are domain-specific, need custom verifiers (often expensive or unreliable), and once written are themselves static — they don't co-evolve with the policy.
    • Mechanism. A layer of plug-in components inserted at the standard environment interface (observation, action, reward, reset). Each component intercepts these calls to reshape the environment's behaviour (state exposure, action affordances, reward shaping, task setup) while the underlying verifier logic is left intact — every reshaped environment inherits the original correctness check, so the reward is still trustworthy.
    • Why. Beats domain-specific environment generators on two axes at once: no per-domain pipeline to maintain, and reshaping stays valid because the verifier is preserved by construction. The alternative — regenerating whole environments from scratch every training iteration — is what makes prior methods brittle and expensive.
  • EnvRigger — the mechanism.
    • Problem. Even with EnvHarness as a substrate, someone has to write the components. Doing this by hand doesn't scale, and doing it heuristically can't target the failure modes actually appearing in current rollouts.
    • Mechanism. Treat the policy as a black box. Roll it out, observe execution trajectories, diagnose recurring flaws (stuck states, off-policy actions, reward hacking, spec ambiguities), then synthesize EnvHarness components that specifically stress those flaws — and validate the synthesized components by re-rolling out on the new wrapped environment before promoting them.
    • Why. The synthesized components are grounded in observed failure modes rather than the environment designer's priors, and the validate-by-fresh-rollout gate blocks components that don't actually reshape behaviour or accidentally invalidate the verifier. This is what enables continuous policy↔environment co-evolution rather than a one-shot generation step.
  • Standard-interface design means EnvHarness applies across diverse domains without domain-specific rewrites — the same wrapper machinery lands on all five evaluated benchmarks.

Evaluation & results

  • Five benchmarks across four domains. Headline numbers: up to +9.0pp accuracy on held-out instances vs. original environments and vs. domain-specific environment-generation baselines. Execution-step count on solved instances drops by 9.8%.
  • Used as an optimization signal for RL fine-tuning, EnvHarness-shaped environments yield strictly better policies than either the static original or the domain-specific generated variants at matched compute — the paper frames this as evidence for targeted policy↔environment co-evolution.

Ablations

  • Verifier-preservation is treated as a design invariant rather than an ablation knob — removing it collapses the trust-the-reward assumption and the shaped environment no longer generalizes to held-out instances.
  • EnvRigger's diagnose-then-synthesize loop is ablated against random component sampling; targeted synthesis is where the held-out gains concentrate.

Other

  • Positioning: this is the third-quarter-2026 continuation of the "environments as first-class training artifacts" thread that Microsoft AI's Agent Lightning (2026-08-21 review) and ByteDance Seed's StartupBench (2026-08-21 review) also inhabit — but EnvHarness is the first to make the environment itself programmable at the wrapper level under a preserved verifier, rather than reshaping only the training loop or the eval rubric.

EXIMO: VLM Guided Exploration of VLA Policies

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-20 VLA finetuning, VLM planner, residual off-policy RL

Overview

  • Bhavya Sukhija, Oliver Groth, Mohit Shridhar, Tim Hertweck, Michael Bloesch, Markus Wulfmeier, Abbas Abdolmaleki, Martin Riedmiller (Google DeepMind). Directly targets the "finetune a billion-parameter VLA for a new manipulation task without recollecting teleoperation data" problem.
  • One named contribution: EXIMO, a three-stage pipeline — Explore, Imitate, Optimize — that couples a VLM planner to the VLA at data-collection time and layers residual off-policy RL on top of the imitation-trained student.

Methodology

  • EXIMO — the mechanism.
    • Problem. Behaviour-cloning a large VLA on a new task needs hundreds of hours of teleoperation, which doesn't scale. Direct RL fine-tuning of a billion-parameter VLA is sample-inefficient on long-horizon tasks, and the VLA's size and architecture make on-policy RL doubly costly (large forward passes per rollout step, gradients through the whole VLA at every update).
    • Mechanism. Three stages executed in order.
      1. Explore. Pair the VLA with a VLM planner. The VLM ingests the long-horizon instruction, decomposes it into shorter sub-instructions, and issues each sub-instruction to the VLA. The joint VLM+VLA system rolls out on the new task and produces an orchestrated dataset — trajectories that are shorter, more tractable, and directly aligned with the sub-task decomposition.
      2. Imitate. Finetune the VLA on the orchestrated data with standard behaviour cloning — giving a task-adapted starting policy without any human teleoperation collection.
      3. Optimize. Apply residual off-policy RL on top of the imitation-tuned VLA. Rather than training the full VLA on-policy, the residual head absorbs task-specific corrections and the buffer of orchestrated + on-task data is reused off-policy.
    • Why. The VLM shoulders long-horizon planning, so the VLA sees only short-horizon sub-tasks where behaviour cloning is data-efficient and where RL credit assignment is tractable. Residual off-policy RL avoids re-training the full VLA on-policy, which is the specific cost point that made prior VLA-RL work impractical. Beats prior VLA finetuning by ablating out each stage — each of the three is shown necessary.

Evaluation & results

  • Ablation over all three stages: removing any stage (Explore, Imitate, or Optimize) significantly hurts both sample efficiency and final task performance. The full three-stage EXIMO dominates on both axes.
  • Positioned against prior VLA finetuning approaches (behaviour cloning only, direct RL only, VLM-augmented BC without residual RL); reports significant improvements without publishing a headline single-number leaderboard in the abstract.

Other

  • Same lab-lineage as prior DeepMind residual-RL and hierarchical-manipulation work (Riedmiller, Abdolmaleki, Wulfmeier); EXIMO reads as the VLA-scale continuation of that thread.

4DAnyone: Create Anyone in 4D from a Casual Monocular Video

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-20 4D Gaussian Splatting, Video Diffusion, Multi-View Consistency

Overview

  • Yudong Jin, Tao Xie, Qihang Zhang, Zehong Shen, Zhen Xu, Yujun Shen, Hujun Bao, Xiaowei Zhou, Yinghao Xu (Ant Research + Zhejiang University collaborators). Reconstructs 4D humans from an uncalibrated monocular video by generating reconstruction-grade multiview-consistent videos and lifting them into 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS).
  • Two named contributions: Reference Context Packing (RCP) and Target Context Routing (TCR), each addressing one side of a bounded-attention-context problem the authors identify in existing camera-controlled video diffusion models. Also ships the MVGameHuman dataset from an in-house game engine.

Methodology

  • Framing: existing camera-controlled video diffusion synthesizes plausible novel-view videos, but the tens of target views 4DGS needs exceed the capacity of a single DiT forward pass. They must be split into groups, which exposes two coupled bottlenecks:
    • Reference-context bottleneck. Conditioning on all previously generated views grows as O(N) tokens — cross-view appearance guidance weakens as the target set grows.
    • Target-context bottleneck. Disjoint groups can't directly exchange information across groups, so global structural drift accumulates.
  • RCP — the mechanism.
    • Problem. Growing reference context breaks cross-view appearance guidance at 4DGS-scale view counts.
    • Mechanism. Compress the growing reference views into a fixed-length, mixed-resolution context cache. Recently added / most-informative references sit at higher resolution; older / redundant references are down-resolved and packed into the same slots. Reference-context complexity per denoising step becomes O(1) instead of O(N).
    • Why. Cross-view appearance guidance is preserved at a bounded compute budget; the model can synthesize the tens of target views 4DGS needs without the guidance signal decaying with N. Beats naive reference-truncation, which drops appearance detail rather than compressing it.
  • TCR — the mechanism.
    • Problem. Disjoint target-view groups can't exchange information within a single DiT forward pass, so global structure drifts across the full 4DGS view set.
    • Mechanism. Rotate target-view groupings across denoising steps as a function of the noise schedule. At high-noise steps (early denoising, where global structure is being resolved), the rotation shares context across groups so structure decisions are consistent. At low-noise steps (late denoising, where per-view details are sharpened), groupings stabilize so per-view details converge without cross-group interference.
    • Why. Uses the denoising schedule itself as the routing signal — high-noise steps trade group-locality for global consistency, low-noise steps trade global consistency for detail sharpness. Fixes the drift that pure fixed-group denoising exhibits without paying for a monolithic all-views forward pass.
  • MVGameHuman dataset. Built in an in-house game engine to give paired multiview supervision at 4DGS scale. Combined at training time with light-stage data and in-the-wild video data for domain mix.

Evaluation & results

  • Evaluated on DNA-Rendering and DyMVHumans. Reports outperforming prior methods on both novel-view video quality and downstream 4DGS reconstruction, with robust in-the-wild generalization from casual monocular input.
  • Project page (with video results and source code): 4danyone.github.io.

Ablations

  • RCP without TCR: reference guidance stays sharp but global structure drifts across groups. TCR without RCP: groups stay globally consistent but appearance guidance decays with N. Only the combination hits both axes.

WithEveryone: Unified Planning and Identity Grounding for Group Image Generation

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-20 Identity-preserving generation, Layout grounding, DiT training

Overview

  • Hengyuan Xu, Qixun Wang, Yiji Cheng, Miles Yang, Zhao Zhong, Wei Cheng, Xingjun Ma, Yu-gang Jiang (Tencent Hunyuan + Fudan collaborators). Framework for generating group images with up to 10 reference identities.
  • Three named contributions: addressed identity tokens, Layout-Grounded ID Loss, and ID Representation Forcing. Ships identity-disjoint benchmark results against GPT-Image-2.

Methodology

  • Addressed identity tokens.
    • Problem. Injecting many reference identities as a bag of embeddings loses the binding from a specific reference to a specific person and location in the target image.
    • Mechanism. Each selected identity is injected into the DiT as an addressed token, then a structured identity–layout plan is predicted and rendered as a visual condition — effectively converting "which identity goes where" into a layout-grounded conditioning signal the diffusion model can attend to per token.
    • Why. Explicit token addressing plus a rendered layout condition beats implicit binding through global cross-attention, which is what fails as identity count grows.
  • Layout-Grounded ID Loss.
    • Problem. Standard identity losses match a predicted face embedding to the reference embedding — but during training the predicted image contains multiple noisy faces, so establishing the correspondence between predicted faces and intended references is itself unstable and error-prone.
    • Mechanism. Use annotated face regions from the training data to supervise the intended identity directly at the intended pixel location. No embedding-based face-matching step at training time — the loss knows which face region is meant to be which identity because the layout supervises it.
    • Why. Removes the failure mode where noisy face matching cross-wires two identities in the same image — the specific pathology behind copy-paste artifacts at high identity counts. Copy-paste artifact rate drops from 0.169 (GPT-Image-2) to 0.055.
  • ID Representation Forcing.
    • Problem. Even with layout grounding, the identity signal can degrade before image synthesis starts — the model can silently drop identities during planning.
    • Mechanism. Train the model to predict a per-identity representation before image synthesis begins, forcing each identity to survive the planning stage as a first-class prediction target rather than a byproduct.
    • Why. Directly attacks the "identity dropped in planning" mode; explains why the duplicate rate stays at 2.8% at coverage 97.3% of requested identities.

FlashPrefill V2: Block-Sparse Prefill Attention for Long-Context LLM Serving

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-20 Long-context serving, Sparse attention, FA3/4-aligned kernels, SGLang

Overview

  • Qihang Fan, Huaibo Huang, Zhiying Wu, Bingning Wang, Ran He (Tencent). Evolves the FlashPrefill prototype into a production-viable long-context serving kernel: block-sparse prefill attention with a mean-correction term, FA3/4-aligned FP8-capable kernels, and native paged-KV / continuous-batching support in SGLang.
  • Three named contributions: mean correction term, FA3/4-aligned kernel redesign, and serving-stack integration. Headline throughput: up to 47.26× vs FA-2 at 128K context (FP8), 27.19× (BF16), and 30.49× over an FA3/4-aligned dense baseline (FP8), on NVIDIA H20.

Methodology

  • Mean correction term.
    • Problem. FlashPrefill's original max-based dynamic thresholding for sparsity control leaves an approximation error that grows with sparsity — at extreme sparsity levels needed for real long-context serving the error becomes a performance-degradation cliff.
    • Mechanism. Add a mean-based correction on top of max-based thresholding, effectively subtracting a moving-mean bias in the sparse-attention approximation so the residual error stays bounded even when the sparsity ratio is aggressive.
    • Why. Lets the kernel stay usable at the sparsity ratios required for real workload throughput without dropping quality — the specific fragility that made FlashPrefill an algorithmic prototype rather than a serving component.
  • FA3/4-aligned kernel redesign.
    • Problem. A prototype sparse-attention kernel that doesn't match FlashAttention-3/4's memory-access and pipelining conventions can't be dropped into a modern serving stack — and can't emit FP8 outputs at the quality LM quantization requires.
    • Mechanism. Reimplement the sparse-attention operator with PackGQA memory access (grouped-query attention layout matching FA3/4), warp specialization (dedicated warps for load/compute/reduce), and pingpong pipelining (double-buffered producer–consumer between shared-memory tiles). Adds an FP8 code path aligned with FA3/4's numerics so integrated quantization is native.
    • Why. Alignment means the sparse operator can substitute for the dense FA3/4 operator inside a serving stack without changing the surrounding memory layout. FP8 alignment is what makes the H20 throughput numbers land at practical quantization.
  • Serving-stack integration.
    • Problem. Prefill attention that ignores paged KV cache or continuous batching can't be an attention backend for real inference frameworks — even a fast kernel is unusable if it forces the stack around it to serialize requests.
    • Mechanism. Native paged-KV cache handling and continuous batching baked into the kernel, wired up as an SGLang attention backend so requests can share the KV budget and stream in and out per-token without pipeline stalls.
    • Why. Turns a fast operator into a fast backend: the throughput numbers below are backend-level, not micro-benchmark, so they translate to actual served-token throughput.
