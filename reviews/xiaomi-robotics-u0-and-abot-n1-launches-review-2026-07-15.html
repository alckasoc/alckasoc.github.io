Alibaba

ABot-N1: Toward a General Visual Language Navigation Foundation Model

Tier 1 · Technical Report arXiv 2026-07-11 Embodied foundation model · Visual-Language Navigation · Slow-fast VLA · GRPO

Overview

  • Authors: Ruiyan Gong, Yingnan Guo, Junjun Hu, et al. (40 authors), corresponding Mu Xu. Affiliation: AMAP CV Lab, Alibaba Group. Companion works from the same lab are cross-linked on the project page: SocialNav (CVPR 26), NavForesee (CVPR 26), FantasyVLN (CVPR 26), AstraNav-Memory (CVPR 26), CE-Nav (ICLR 26), OmniNav (ICLR 26).
  • ABot-N1 is positioned as a step toward a general Visual Language Navigation foundation model that unifies five navigation paradigms behind one interface: point-goal (reach metric coordinates), POI-goal (navigate to a Point-of-Interest entrance), object-goal (open-vocabulary object search), instruction-following (long-horizon natural-language paths), and person-following (dynamic human target tracking).
  • Architecture: an asynchronous slow-fast dual-system factorization — a slow vision-language reasoner (Qwen-3.5-4B-VL) emits explicit CoT plus a pixel goal at low frequency, and a fast action expert (Qwen-3.5-2B) consumes those anchors plus real-time observations and emits continuous SE(2) waypoints at native control frequency.
  • Headline results: 77.3% SR@2m on ABotN-POIBench (+35.0 over POINav 42.3%), 95.4% / 92.9% SR on ABotN-PointBench indoor / outdoor All, 84.9 / 51.8 SR / SPL on VLN-CE R2R, 70.9 / 67.5 on VLN-CE RxR (Qwen-VLA 57.3 / 51.2), and 3.32 NE on RxR (best NavFoM 4.61).
  • Two new open-source benchmarks released with the paper: ABotN-PointBench (31 real-world scenes, 465 reference trajectories, closed-loop SR / SPL) and ABotN-POIBench (11 commercial regions, 163 POIs, 3DGS reconstruction, physical entrance annotations, SR@2m).
  • Pre-training data corpus: 30M trajectories, 8,423 3D scenes, 14.7 km2 total area coverage, 482 km of paths, aggregated across all five task paradigms.

Architecture

Slow-Fast Dual-System with Pixel-Goal Interface.

  • Problem. Prior VLN foundation models — monolithic policies that map observations directly to actions — suffer from coordinate drift (regressing metric waypoints from pixels is under-constrained), poor long-tail semantics (a single black-box policy cannot maintain interpretable grounding across POI-goal, object-goal, instruction-following, and person-following simultaneously), and zero interpretability, so failure modes cannot be diagnosed and cross-task generality can’t be enforced.
  • Mechanism. Cognition and control are split into two asynchronous VLMs joined by an image-space interface. The slow system (Qwen-3.5-4B-VL) processes historical frames plus the task prompt at low frequency and emits (a) a natural-language CoT trace and (b) two pixel-space anchors — a target pixel and an affordance pixel. The fast system (Qwen-3.5-2B backbone) jointly encodes real-time tri-view observations, the CoT trace, and the cached pixel anchors; a learnable action query attends to the output hidden states through a QFormer module, and an MLP decodes the queries into a sequence of continuous SE(2) waypoints. When the slow system is deliberating, the fast system continues to track the last cached pixel goal, so the control loop never blocks.
  • Why. Pixel goals are the single interface that generalizes across all five tasks: they can encode a metric point (project it into pixels), a POI entrance (detect the storefront), an object goal (segment the target), a next milestone of an instruction, and a person to follow. Compared with prior VLA policies that regress waypoints from pixels directly, the discrete pixel-goal decision made by the slow system concentrates the future-waypoint distribution into a dominant mode, which turns the fast policy’s waypoint regression into an approximately uni-modal smooth-L1 problem — avoiding the mode collapse that vanilla waypoint regression exhibits on multi-modal targets. Making the two systems asynchronous rather than serial is what lets a 4B-parameter CoT reasoner co-exist with a native-frequency control loop.

Pre-training

  • Both systems initialize from pretrained Qwen-3.5 checkpoints (4B-VL and 2B). The slow system is trained with cross-entropy over CoT tokens and pixel-coordinate tokens; the fast system is trained with smooth-L1 on position and heading angle plus a binary arriving loss.
  • The 30M-trajectory pretraining corpus is stratified per navigation paradigm: point-goal (CoT with affordance-pixel + target-perturbation, plus sub-optimal / OOD-correction VLA trajectories); POI-goal (three-stage pipeline — geometric seed via monocular depth → 31M street-view pairs distilled by a VLM down to 8M valid paths → tri-view episodes with positive / negative rejection pairs); object-goal (an iterative flywheel of VLM-seeded CoT rationales, A* consistency filtering, self-play harvesting, yielding 110K structured samples); instruction-following (long instructions decomposed into sub-instructions aligned with milestone frame ranges, then affordance / target pixel generation and verification); person-following (avatar trajectories → A* waypoint planning → visibility detection → stochastic perturbation for CoT; sub-optimal / OOD-correction for VLA).
  • Both systems are trained with noise-injected inputs — perturbed target coordinates, noisy prior pixel predictions, simulated asynchronous lag — specifically to make the fast system robust to imperfect slow-system outputs at deployment.

Post-training

Task-agnostic GRPO alignment.

  • Problem. Applying preference-based post-training to a navigation VLA runs into two failure modes: (1) biased preference sampling collapses the diversity of the pretrained policy; (2) sparse-reward RL yields reward-hacking equilibria — the policy learns to withhold predictions or take permanent inaction, both of which trivially satisfy safety reward while failing the task.
  • Mechanism. Post-training uses GRPO with an action-balanced batch sampling scheme (each batch is balanced across the discrete action space so preference gradients are unbiased across action modes) and a decomposed reward R = Rformat + Rtarget + Rsafety. Format and safety rewards are constructed to be task-agnostic: Rformat depends only on the output schema; Rsafety depends only on scene geometry and traversability rules. Reward-hacking is defused with a strong explicit penalty for missing predictions, so target-withholding is dominated by the target reward. Extending the pipeline to a new task requires only redefining Rtarget.
  • Why. The task-agnostic factoring is what makes the pipeline actually scale to five paradigms — the same GRPO run works across point-goal, POI-goal, object-goal, instruction-following, and person-following without re-designing the reward loop. Action-balanced sampling avoids the standard GRPO failure of over-representing common actions and killing behavioral diversity. The pipeline was validated at scale on 0.5M point-goal episodes before being reused for the other paradigms.

Safety & Limitations

  • Safety is folded directly into the GRPO reward as Rsafety, which depends only on scene geometry and traversability rules. Because it’s task-agnostic, the same safety term applies across all five paradigms and prevents cross-task interference where post-training on one navigation mode degrades safe behaviour in another.
  • The action-balanced batch sampling plus strong “missing prediction” penalty are engineered against two specific reward-hacking equilibria observed during development: target withholding (the policy learns to not commit to a target because non-commitment is not penalized) and permanent inaction (safety reward without target reward is trivially satisfied by standing still).
  • The paper is described as a “step toward” a general VLN foundation model rather than a full generalist — the five paradigms are still task-typed at input, and cross-task compositional prompts (e.g. “find object X inside POI Y”) aren’t evaluated.

ABot-AgentOS: A General Robotic Agent OS with Lifelong Multi-modal Memory

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-07-11 Robotic agent OS · Multi-modal memory · EmbodiedWorldBench

Overview

  • Authors: Jiayi Tian, Shiao Liu, Yuting Xu, Jia Lu et al., corresponding Jiaheng Liu. AMAP CV Lab, Alibaba Group. Companion release to ABot-N1.
  • ABot-AgentOS is a runtime layer that sits above low-level VLM / VLA controllers and provides a deliberative agent layer — scene-conditioned planning, context-isolated skill execution, multi-stage verification, multi-modal memory, and edge-cloud collaboration.
  • Accompanied by EmbodiedWorldBench, an executable benchmark with 16 indoor / outdoor / hybrid scenes, four difficulty levels, and 200+ tasks spanning navigation, object search, NPC dialogue, dynamic events, and trace-grounded scoring.
  • Static memory results: 87.5 LoCoMo, 59.9 OpenEQA EM-EQA, 88.6 Mem-Gallery, 76.5 Acc@All on NExT-QA. Self-evolution lifts LoCoMo to 88.7, OpenEQA to 60.4, Mem-Gallery to 89.0.

Methodology

Universal Multi-modal Graph Memory + Failure-Driven Self-Evolution.

  • Problem. Long-horizon embodied agents need a persistent memory that survives across episodes and integrates dialogue, visual observations, spatial context, temporal relations, and task traces — but plugging that memory in over an evaluation benchmark creates a leakage risk: the same agent that failed on split N and stored a corrective memory can trivially recover on the same split at eval time.
  • Mechanism. Universal Multi-modal Graph Memory converts each modality — dialogue, vision, spatial context, temporal relations, task traces — into typed nodes and edges in a single graph, source-grounded so provenance is preserved for auditing. A failure-driven self-evolution loop takes diagnosed memory failures and converts them into gated runtime evo-assets that are promoted only into later evaluation splits — the split where the failure occurred is frozen — which prevents current-split ground-truth leakage while still enabling continual improvement across splits.
  • Why. The split-gating discipline is what lets a memory-augmented agent be evaluated fairly on a fixed benchmark. Typed nodes & edges over a single graph are more auditable than the alternative — per-modality memory silos — because cross-modal grounding queries (“which prior frame corresponds to this task-trace entry?”) become a single graph walk rather than an ad-hoc modality bridge.

Evaluation & Results

  • Improves over a single-controller baseline in both task success and goal completion on an initial EmbodiedWorldBench subset (paper does not include a full leaderboard).
  • Memory benchmark scores under “Static” vs. “Self-Evolution”: LoCoMo 87.5 → 88.7; OpenEQA EM-EQA 59.9 → 60.4; Mem-Gallery 88.6 → 89.0; NExT-QA Acc@All 76.5 (self-evolution number not reported in the abstract).

Xiaomi-Robotics-U0: Unified Embodied Synthesis with World Foundation Model

Tier 1 · Technical Report arXiv 2026-07-13 Embodied foundation model · Multi-modal AR · World model · Multi-view generation

Overview

  • Authors: Xiaomi Robotics team — Xinghang Li, Jun Guo, Qiwei Li, Long Qian, Hang Lai et al. (30+ authors), correspondence to mi-robotics@xiaomi.com.
  • Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 is a 38B-parameter multimodal autoregressive world foundation model for unified embodied synthesis. It jointly optimizes five generation tasks under a single next-token-prediction objective: (1) Text-to-Image (T2I), (2) Any-to-Image (X2I — 1–3 reference images + text, i.e. image editing), (3) embodied scene generation (multi-view robot observations from an embodiment + scene description), (4) embodied transfer (multi-view depth → multi-view RGB conditioned on a scene description), and (5) embodied video generation (initial observation + task → 15–25 RGB frames).
  • Initialized from open-source EMU3.5, which is itself built on the Qwen-3-32B decoder-only Transformer plus an IBQ visual tokenizer (16×16 spatial compression) so image and text tokens share one vocabulary. Reported as “the first model to support high-quality multi-view scene generation across multiple robot embodiments” and to “introduce structured, controllable embodied transfer for fine-grained editing while preserving multi-view consistency and interaction dynamics.”
  • Corpus: 9.5M single-step samples (56.4B tokens) + 2.6M video clips (49.6B tokens), sourced across six domains — general image-text (ShareGPT4V), embodied manipulation (AgiBotWorld-Beta, Open X-Embodiment, in-house MiBot multi-view, RoboTwin2.0, GenieSim, InternData-A1), autonomous driving (Cosmos-Drive-Dreams), egocentric (EgoWalk), 3D reconstruction (ScanNet++ v2, DL3DV-10K), and game data (Open-P2P).
  • Headline results: outperforms GPT-Image-2 in human evaluations of embodied scene generation and embodied transfer; ranks first on World Arena for embodied video generation; lifts the OOD success rate of a downstream π0.5 policy from 36.9% → 63.2% on challenging real-world manipulation tasks. FlashAR+ inference is up to 82.9× faster than standard AR at 1024×1024 T2I; with vLLM integration the latency drops to 5.44 s / image on a single H20 (450.77 s baseline).
  • Two open checkpoints on Hugging Face: XiaomiRobotics/Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 (labeled 34B) and XiaomiRobotics/Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-FlashAR (labeled 38B).

Architecture

Unified NTP world foundation model over text, image, and embodied tokens.

  • Problem. Existing embodied world models adapt a pre-trained foundation image / video model by continued training on robot-specific datasets only. Because embodied datasets are much smaller, less diverse, and highly repetitive relative to the internet-scale visual corpora the foundation model was trained on, this “robot-only” adaptation sacrifices the semantic understanding, controllability, and open-vocabulary generalization that made the base model useful in the first place, and produces embodied outputs with poor multi-view consistency, implausible robot states, and incompatibility with robot control.
  • Mechanism. Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 initializes from EMU3.5 (Qwen-3-32B decoder + IBQ tokenizer). All five tasks — T2I, X2I, embodied scene generation, embodied transfer, embodied video generation — are reformulated as sequences of unified discrete tokens (text, IBQ image tokens, robot-related control tokens) and optimized under a single joint next-token-prediction objective. No task-specific prediction heads. During continued training the T2I / X2I data is co-trained with embodied data specifically to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the pretrained generative capability; embodied scene generation supervises the model to synthesize physically plausible initial robot observations conditioned on embodiment + scene description; embodied transfer supervises cross-scene multi-view generation from multi-view depth conditions.
  • Why. Sharing a single AR head across all five tasks is what allows visual-generation priors (materials, lighting, textures, object geometry) to actually reach the embodied tasks — the alternative, task-specific heads, forces each task to relearn those priors from a much smaller embodied subset. Co-training against T2I / X2I is a direct mechanism against catastrophic forgetting of the pretrained foundation model. Beating GPT-Image-2 on the embodied-transfer benchmark (SI-RMSE 0.14 vs. 0.40) demonstrates that GPT-Image-2’s failure mode — visually plausible but geometrically inconsistent images across views — is exactly what this joint objective fixes: the depth conditions become just another modality inside the same NTP loss.

Structured control decomposition for embodied transfer.

  • Problem. Naive text conditioning for embodied scene generation entangles workspace, background, foreground irrelevant objects, target objects, and lighting into a single caption — which means any augmentation that changes one dimension (e.g. randomize the background) drags along unwanted changes to the others (target-object identity, lighting geometry, workspace layout).
  • Mechanism. Structured Embodied Scene Annotation partitions the scene into five orthogonal semantic dimensions — workspace, task objects, irrelevant objects, lighting, and background — each with its own text description generated by Qwen3-VL-235B. At inference the five dimensions can be independently swapped and re-composed, producing controllable augmentations that keep geometry and interaction dynamics stable.
  • Why. This is what turns the model into a scalable data engine: for each real trajectory, 1000 alternative descriptions per non-task-object dimension are generated and randomly composed, and the resulting augmentations preserve the multi-view geometric consistency that a downstream manipulation policy needs. Concretely, this augmentation pipeline is what lifts π0.5 from 36.9% to 63.2% on OOD real-world manipulation.

FlashAR+ inference extension.

  • Problem. Serial token-by-token generation over a 1024×1024 IBQ latent grid is intolerably slow for AR image generation — 450.77 s per image on a single H20 in the standard AR baseline.
  • Mechanism. FlashAR+ extends FlashAR’s post-training / inference paradigm from single-image T2I to full X2I (T2I, I2I, multi-reference). During training the model treats text + reference images + control conditions + target image header as a fixed conditional prefix (loss is applied only on target tokens; references are never supervised). The target image is generated in anti-diagonal steps — tokens sharing r+c = step are decoded in parallel via a horizontal head and a vertical head fused by a per-position learnable gate g(r,c) ∈ [0,1]. A step-causal 2D attention mask inside the target region prevents information leakage between same-step tokens. The loss is ℒfuse + 0.05ℒh + 0.05ℒv + 0.2ℒdistill; the small 0.05ℒh and 0.05ℒv auxiliary losses explicitly supervise the horizontal and vertical heads to prevent H/V gate collapse toward an under-trained branch.
  • Why. FlashAR+ drops latency to 16.56 s / image (27.22× over standard AR), and vLLM integration (batched execution + paged KV cache + a diagonal scheduler synchronizing CFG conditional / unconditional requests) drops it further to 5.44 s / image at max_num_seq=28 — a 3.04× additional speedup, totaling ~82.9× over baseline AR. The auxiliary H / V losses are the design detail that keeps the gated fusion stable: without them the gate collapses toward the H branch under longer training, and the fused prediction degenerates into a single-direction (unstable) prediction.

Pre-training

  • Data volume. 9.5M single-step samples (56.4B tokens) after autonomous filtering (motion blur, image-quality detection, gripper-state alignment), plus 2.6M video clips (49.6B tokens) for sequential generation.
  • Unified annotation pipeline. Uses Qwen3-VL-235B to produce dense structured captions along four decoupled branches: (1) General scene captions after temporal downsampling on all videos; (2) Structured embodied scene captions with the five-dimension decomposition (workspace / task objects / irrelevant objects / lighting / background); (3) Geometric-modality extraction of temporally-consistent inverse depth via Video Depth Anything; (4) Trajectory-level subtask decomposition via HDBSCAN clustering on end-effector poses and gripper signals to segment motion / grasp transitions.
  • Data balancing. Fine-grained hierarchical grouping over task goals, scene contexts, target objects, spatial relations, subtask phases, and atomic manipulation skills. Downsample redundant embodiment-task-object combinations; up-weight long-tail categories (rare objects, seldom-used skills, core interaction phases — grasping, releasing, placing, object state transitions).
  • Training curriculum. Two setups. Single-step training jointly trains T2I + X2I + embodied scene generation + embodied transfer, using the general T2I / X2I data as an anchor against catastrophic forgetting. Sequential training extends the model to long-horizon behaviour: image-text interleaved subtask-subgoal sequences (each language subtask paired with the multi-view observations after execution) plus embodied manipulation videos at multiple frame rates (FPS 1, 3, 5), where sparse frames drive long-horizon planning and dense frames drive fine-grained interaction dynamics.

Safety & Limitations

  • The main practical limitation is inference cost at 38B and the reliance on EMU3.5 initialization — the FlashAR+ speedup measurements are reported only for T2I / X2I at 1024×1024, not for full embodied video sequences.
  • The zero-shot augmentation is validated on four tasks and one downstream policy (π0.5); how the 36.9 → 63.2 gain generalizes across policy classes and manipulation task families is not reported.
  • No red-team safety card is provided in the technical report; the model is a visual world generator without action outputs, so safety concerns are those of foundation image / video models (e.g., generated content policy) rather than robot-action safety.

Overview

  • Authors: Shiyuan Feng, Huan-ang Gao, Haohan Chi, Hanlin Wu, Zhilong Zhang, Zheng Jiang, Bingxiang He, Wei-Ying Ma, Ya-Qin Zhang, Hao Zhou. From the SIA-Lab, a joint lab of Tsinghua AIR and ByteDance Seed. Project page: bytedtsinghua-sia.github.io/Direct-OPD.
  • Motivation: RLVR (RL with verifiable rewards) on frontier LLMs is compute-dominated by rollouts — re-running RL on every new stronger target model is prohibitive because the target itself must generate the training rollouts. As target scale grows, post-training compute is the bottleneck.
  • Direct-OPD introduces a weak-to-strong alternative: run RL on a small model where rollouts are cheap; reuse what that RL run learned to improve a stronger target model. Simple distillation from the post-RL small model is not enough — the small teacher’s final policy mixes useful RL gains with the limitations of being a small model, so imitating it can drag a stronger student down.
  • Empirical headline: on AIME 2024, Direct-OPD boosts Qwen3-1.7B from 48.3% to 58.3% in 4 hours on 8×A100. In a second setting: 1.5B teacher pair (post-RL teacher score 51.3), 7B student before transfer 56.7; vanilla on-policy distillation of the post-RL teacher drops the student to ~50, while Direct-OPD lifts it to 63.1 (+6.4). Outperforms step-matched direct RL on the target and admits sequential composition of multiple policy shifts.

Methodology

Policy shift as an implicit reward — distill the delta, not the teacher.

  • Problem. The obvious weak-to-strong route — run RLVR on a small model, then distill the resulting post-RL policy into a stronger target — fails when the target is already stronger than the post-RL small teacher. Vanilla on-policy distillation drags the target policy toward the teacher’s ceiling (see the AIME setting where a 56.7% 7B is pulled down to ~50 by imitating a 51.3% teacher). The teacher’s final policy mixes RL gains with small-model limitations; the imitator inherits both.
  • Mechanism. Compare the post-RL small teacher πpost against its own pre-RL reference πpre, treat the log-ratio log πpost(a|s) − log πpre(a|s) as a dense implicit reward that identifies which actions RL made the small model more (or less) likely to take, and apply that reward on the stronger student’s own on-policy states. The student never has to imitate the small teacher’s absolute policy; it only inherits the RL-induced direction. This turns the pair (πpre, πpost) into the reusable RL artifact, not πpost alone.
  • Why. The pre-RL vs. post-RL log-ratio is exactly the sufficient statistic for “what RL taught this model” that is orthogonal to the model’s absolute capability — it captures direction of change, not the level. Applying that signal on the student’s own rollouts is what preserves the student’s superior starting policy while still letting it inherit the small-model’s RL discovery. Compared with distilling the post-RL teacher (which pulls the student toward the teacher’s ceiling) and with step-matched direct RL on the target (which pays the full rollout cost), Direct-OPD both wins on final AIME score and dominates on wall-clock training time.

Evaluation & Results

  • AIME 2024 headline. Qwen3-1.7B: 48.3% → 58.3% (+10.0) in 4 hours on 8×A100 GPUs.
  • Cross-scale reuse. 1.5B teacher pair (pre-RL → post-RL, post-RL scored at 51.3); a 7B student that scored 56.7 before transfer degrades to ~50 under vanilla on-policy distillation, but reaches 63.1 (+6.4) under Direct-OPD.
  • Comparison to step-matched direct RL. Direct-OPD outperforms step-matched direct RL on the target model — a real-world compute win, since running RL on the small model + Direct-OPD is cheaper per step than running RL on the target.
  • Sequential composition. Multiple (πpre, πpost) policy-shift pairs from different weak-model RL runs can be composed on the same student, i.e., the RL discoveries stack.

Other

  • The framing reinterprets what a completed RLVR run is worth: not just the final checkpoint, but the pair (πpre, πpost) as a reusable, model-agnostic supervision signal — a claim directly relevant to the compounding post-training compute bill at frontier scale.
