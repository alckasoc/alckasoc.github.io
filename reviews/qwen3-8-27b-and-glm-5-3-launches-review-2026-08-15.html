  • Sourcing: model card at huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B; Qwen Cloud page for the hosted variant; the Qwen3.8 Collection (4 items, listed as updated 2 days ago). No arXiv technical report yet; the model card is the primary source and includes complete architecture parameters, sampling recipes, quantization strategy, and benchmark grid.
  • Positioning: the tagline is "the most capable generation in the Qwen open-model family to date." Explicit design targets are coding, professional work, research, and long-horizon agentic tasks. Qwen3.8-27B is the "compact, deployment-friendly" member of the family: a native VL model with flexible thinking control, sized to sit against Muse Glimmer-30B and dense open competitors around the ~30B mark rather than against Opus 4.6 Max or GPT-5.6 Sol directly (Qwen3.8-Max fills that slot).

Architecture

  • Overall shape. Causal Language Model with a fused vision encoder, hidden dimension 5120, 64 transformer layers, token embedding padded to 248,320, feed-forward intermediate 17,408, LM head padded to 248,320. Native context 262,144, extensible to 1,000,000 via YaRN. Config identifier qwen3_5 (the architecture family is inherited from Qwen3.5, not renamed for 3.8).
  • Hybrid attention layout. The 64-layer stack is organized as 16 × ( 3 × (Gated DeltaNet → FFN) + 1 × (Gated Attention → FFN) ). That is, three linear-attention DeltaNet layers followed by one full gated-attention layer, repeated 16 times.
    • Gated DeltaNet (linear-attention block): 48 V heads and 16 QK heads at head-dim 128.
    • Gated Attention (full-attention block): 24 Q heads and 4 KV heads at head-dim 256, with a partial RoPE of dimension 64.
    • Problem → Mechanism → Why. Full-attention layers throughout the stack would blow up KV-cache size at the intended ~1M-token context; a pure linear-attention stack loses the exact-recall capacity needed for coding and long-context reasoning. The 3:1 hybrid ratio keeps DeltaNet's cheap sequence-length scaling on the majority of layers while retaining one true attention layer per block for exact recall — giving the model roughly the memory footprint of a hybrid-linear architecture at the reasoning quality of a mostly-attention architecture. The gated-DeltaNet variant (vs vanilla DeltaNet) also lets the linear layer approximate more expressive kernels without giving up parallel training.
  • Multi-Token Prediction (MTP). The head is trained with multiple lookahead steps — the model card records this as trained with multiple steps. Problem → Mechanism → Why. Standard next-token prediction underuses the top hidden state as a supervision signal; training with multi-step MTP means each hidden state is regressed against several future tokens, which both regularizes representations and pre-conditions the checkpoint for downstream speculative decoding and (per the Qwen Cloud page) built-in draft-and-verify inference.
  • Vision-language. Native image and video inputs. Video preprocessing is conservatively configured out of the box (size.longest_edge tuned for text-heavy workloads); the model card provides a recommended override to longest_edge: 469,762,048 (equivalent to 224K video tokens) to enable higher frame-rate sampling for hour-scale videos, with vLLM and SGLang PR references (vLLM #34330, SGLang #18467) for the implementation. Sampling parameter fps (default 2) is user-tunable in vLLM.
  • Long-context recipe. Native 262K; extension to 1M is done via YaRN with concrete parameters shipped in the model card: 
    {
  "mrope_interleaved": true,
  "mrope_section": [11, 11, 10],
  "rope_type": "yarn",
  "rope_theta": 10000000,
  "partial_rotary_factor": 0.25,
  "factor": 4.0,
  "original_max_position_embeddings": 262144
}
    The card explicitly warns that static YaRN degrades short-context quality and recommends only enabling the extension when actually needed, and tuning factor to the workload (e.g. 2.0 for 524K use cases).

Post-training and inference-time controls

  • Thinking mode is on by default. A <think>\n...</think>\n\n block precedes the response. Callers can disable it per-request via chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking=False or, on Qwen Cloud, enable_thinking=False directly.
  • Named contribution — reasoning_effort.
    • Problem. A single fixed thinking budget forces callers to overpay compute on easy prompts or underpay on hard ones; ad-hoc "think longer" prompts drift.
    • Mechanism. The API exposes three named tiers — xhigh (default), medium, low — each mapping to an internal reasoning-length regime baked into post-training so the model actually stops on time in the lower tiers.
    • Why. Compared to prompt-level "think for N tokens" hacks, tier-conditioning is a learned property so the low tier does not just truncate but produces genuinely shorter reasoning; the model card notes that in multi-turn agentic tasks lower effort can increase total task time because thinner analysis leads to more retries.
  • Named contribution — preserve_thinking.
    • Problem. Standard chat-completion serialization strips the assistant's thinking blocks from prior turns, forcing the model to re-derive intermediate state on every turn and breaking KV-cache reuse across turns.
    • Mechanism. When preserve_thinking=True (the default), thinking blocks from every historical assistant turn are retained in the context sent to the model on the next turn.
    • Why. For agent scenarios — multi-turn tool-use loops in particular — keeping the reasoning trace gives the model full history, avoids re-deriving state, and (per the model card) improves KV-cache utilization at inference. The trade is context window; callers can flip to per-turn-latest-only via preserve_thinking=False.
  • Sampling defaults baked into the shipped recipe:
    • Thinking mode: temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95, top_k=20, min_p=0.0, presence_penalty=0.0, repetition_penalty=1.0.
    • Instruct (non-thinking) mode: temperature=0.7, top_p=0.80, top_k=20, min_p=0.0, presence_penalty=1.5, repetition_penalty=1.0.
    • Recommended output budget within the 1M context: reasoning up to 262,144 tokens, final response up to 131,072 tokens.

NVIDIA

Context-Matched Distillation: Teacher Causality for Autoregressive Video Distillation

Tier 2 · arXiv arXiv:2608.13391 · cs.CV 2026-08-13 Video generation · Distillation · Autoregressive · Interactive control

Overview

  • Authors: Hmrishav Bandyopadhyay, Xuanchi Ren, Zijian Huang, Jay Zhangjie Wu, Tianshi Cao, Ruilong Li, Bryan Chu, Sanja Fidler, Yi-Zhe Song, Zian Wang. Project page at hmrishavbandy.github.io/cmd-site/. Submitted 2026-08-13 (v1), 6,185 KB source. Author affiliations map to NVIDIA (organization tag on the Hugging Face Daily Papers listing) with academic co-authors.
  • Setting: interactive autoregressive video generation. Two competing constraints: (1) low-latency rollouts demand a few-step distilled student; (2) online control (camera path, actions) demands a causal information set — frames and blocks may depend only on history and controls available at generation time.
  • Problem statement: existing video distribution-matching-distillation (DMD) pipelines distill causal few-step students using bidirectional teachers that score complete clips. The teacher's score for a target can therefore depend on future frames and controls that were not available when the student generated that target — a supervision-side leakage of the future into training a causal model. CMD reframes DMD to close this leak.

Methodology

  • Named contribution — causal teacher for causal students.
    • Problem. A bidirectional teacher scores each target given the entire clip, so its gradient for target frame t depends on frames > t. When that gradient trains a causal student that at inference sees only frames ≤ t, the student is supervised for a task it cannot perform — and predictably fails to obey time-varying controls.
    • Mechanism. Replace the bidirectional full-clip scorer with a causal teacher that scores each target using only history and current controls, exactly matching the student's information set. The same causal teacher is used to initialize the few-step student, so the causal formulation is consistent across teacher training, distillation, and inference.
    • Why. Alignment on the temporal information boundary means the DMD gradient is a legitimate distributional-match signal for the student's actual generation task, rather than a signal that requires access the student will never have. This is the smallest change that makes DMD sound for autoregressive video.
  • Named contribution — Prefix Scoring.
    • Problem. Even a causal teacher, applied to a target frame, is normally conditioned on the ground-truth prefix from the training clip. But at inference the student's prefix is whatever the student itself rolled out — often meaningfully different in early training. Scoring a target under the ground-truth prefix trains the student to hit targets that never appear along its own trajectory.
    • Mechanism. Prefix Scoring evaluates each target under the cached student-generated prefix that produced it. The teacher gradient is thus computed over the exact context the student's rollout actually encountered.
    • Why. Matches the supervision to the student's realized rollout context, closing the second leak (ground-truth-prefix leakage) after the causal-teacher fix closes the first (future-frame leakage). Both are needed to make the distillation objective consistent with autoregressive inference.
  • Named contribution — Prefix Corruption.
    • Problem. Early in training, student-generated prefixes are unreliable — noisy enough that Prefix Scoring on top of them destabilizes gradients and can collapse the student to trivial modes.
    • Mechanism. Prefix Corruption perturbs unreliable early-training prefixes while preserving the target-context alignment property. As training proceeds and prefixes improve, corruption is annealed.
    • Why. Preserves the DMD stability benefits of a stationary supervision distribution during early training while still producing a student conditioned on realistic rollout prefixes at convergence.
  • Extensions. The causal formulation naturally scales to frame-wise and chunk-wise generation, long-video distillation, and camera-conditioned distillation without changing the objective.

Evaluation & results

  • The paper reports state-of-the-art aggregate performance among autoregressive methods on both short- and long-video benchmarks (the arXiv abstract summarizes results at the aggregate level; per-benchmark tables live in the PDF and are not reproduced here to avoid mis-numbering).
  • Headline qualitative claim: substantially improved adherence to time-varying camera controls. This is the specific failure mode that the bidirectional-teacher supervision introduced (the student was gradient-punished when it obeyed a control the teacher, seeing the future, thought would be relaxed), and it is the direct payoff of the causal-teacher fix.

Notes

  • This work is the second frontier-lab DMD-for-video result in the last month after AMAP-ML's DreamX-Phi 1.0, which also used distribution-matching distillation to reach few-step deployment latency for an action-conditioned world model. CMD and DreamX-Phi share the underlying DMD framework but target different failure modes: DreamX-Phi's mechanism was action-conditioning stability via SAM3+V-JEPA object-identity teachers, while CMD's is temporal information-boundary alignment for interactive camera control.
