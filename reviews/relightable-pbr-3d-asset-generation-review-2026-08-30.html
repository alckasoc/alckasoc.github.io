The tangent-space normal map transfer lifts mesh normal PSNR from 29.5 to 33.0 dB "at zero polygon-count cost."

Competitiveness

Luce's peer group is the recent structured-latent generative 3D line — TRELLIS, TRELLIS 2, LiTo, 3DTopia-XL — and, more broadly, per-Gaussian PBR generators (TexGaussian, RelitLRM, MGM, MatSpray). The paper's argument for beating them has three prongs, each attached to a named mechanism. First, per-modality Gaussian sets vs. a single shared set (or PBR attributes attached to a single Gaussian cloud) let woodgrain-rich albedo and near-uniform normals not fight for the same primitives — the FID advantage on Toys4K comes from this decomposition. Second, multi-layer DINOv2 conditioning (early spatial + deep semantic) vs. single-layer conditioning preserves the legible text, logos, and inscriptions that other image-to-3D systems distort; the same ablation reports FID 20.99 vs. 25.21 and CLIP 0.8519 vs. 0.8081 on the AI-image benchmark. Luce: Relightable Gaussians for 3D Asset Generation

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-08-25 Image-to-3D generation · PBR · Gaussian splatting · Rectified flow · Relighting

Overview

  • Mayank Singh, Michele Stoppa, Alvise Memo, Rui Yu, Harsha Kalli, Srimanth Gunturi, Muhammad Ahmed Riaz, Behrooz Shahsavari, Waleed Abdulla, David E. Jacobs — all Apple. arXiv:2608.23943v1 [cs.CV], 27 pages, 19 figures, 5 tables, submitted 2026-08-25.
  • The framing: high-fidelity image-to-3D generation needs a representation that captures geometry and physically based rendering (PBR) materials — albedo, metallic-roughness, surface normals — so the asset can be relit under a novel environment map and dropped into standard rendering pipelines. Prior structured-latent generators either bake lighting into the representation (TRELLIS-style DINOv2-feature voxels, appearance-only Gaussian splatting variants) or attach PBR attributes as a single per-Gaussian bundle (TRELLIS 2's mesh-centric O-Voxels, TexGaussian's mesh-conditioned attachments), forcing every material modality onto one primitive layout.
  • Luce introduces four contributions: (i) a multimodal PBR Gaussian representation with per-modality Gaussian sets; (ii) a unified latent (SLatVAE) that jointly encodes geometry and all material modalities; (iii) multi-layer DINOv2 image conditioning; (iv) tangent-space normal map transfer that bakes decoded normal Gaussians onto the extracted mesh as tangent-space normal maps.

Methodology

  • Named contribution 1 — Multimodal PBR Gaussian representation with per-modality independent Gaussian sets.
    • Problem. Attaching PBR attributes (albedo, metallic-roughness, normal) as extra channels on a single per-Gaussian bundle forces all three modalities to share one geometric layout — positions, scales, rotations, opacities. But polished wood needs dense albedo Gaussians for woodgrain while its normals are nearly smooth; brushed metal needs dense metallic-roughness and normal Gaussians for scratches over nearly uniform albedo. A shared layout wastes primitives where they're not needed and starves modalities where they are.
    • Mechanism. Each occupied voxel i carries three independent Gaussian sets — Gialb, Gimr, Ginor — each with its own (•ijm: p, s, q, o, c). The per-voxel bundle is Fi = {Gim}m∈{alb,mr,nor}. Each modality is splatted independently via standard 3DGS alpha-compositing to per-pixel • (albedo a, metallic κ, roughness α, normal n, opacity o); normals are renormalized to unit length after compositing. Shading uses split-sum image-based lighting with the Cook–Torrance microfacet BRDF: LoIBL(ωo) ≈ (1-κ)a/π⋅E(n) + Lpref(r,α)⋅[F0A(μo,α) + B(μo,α)] with F0 = (1-κ)⋅0.04 + κ⋅a. Unlike original 3DGS the representation stores no spherical harmonics; view-dependent shading is produced analytically through PBR compositing.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Per-modality independence lets each modality allocate primitives where its detail lives, so woodgrain and smooth-normals no longer compete. The FID advantage on Toys4K (20.99 vs. TRELLIS 2's 29.22) tracks this: prior single-bundle PBR (TRELLIS 2) and appearance-only Gaussian splatting (TRELLIS) both bake either lighting or a shared layout into the representation, and the FID gap widens exactly on assets where the modalities have different frequency structure.
  • Named contribution 2 — SLatVAE: structured latent VAE that trades spatial resolution for per-voxel Gaussian density.
    • Problem. The raw multimodal Gaussian cloud 𝒱 is high-dimensional and sparsely structured. Modeling it directly with a generative model is computationally expensive; but downsampling the voxel grid without compensation drops surface detail below what the PBR shading can carry.
    • Mechanism. The SLatVAE encoder ℰ is a sparse transformer with shifted-window attention that downsamples the voxel grid from resolution d to a coarser d′ < d, producing z = ℰ(𝒱) ∈ ℝNvd′3 × C. The decoder 𝒟 runs at the coarser latent resolution for efficiency and offsets the lost spatial resolution by predicting more Gaussians per voxel per modality. Training is end-to-end with a rendering-based reconstruction loss (as in TRELLIS): decoded Gaussians are differentiably rendered per modality against the ground-truth intrinsic images, with a KL prior and light regularizers on scale and opacity.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. The per-voxel-density trade-off keeps the latent small enough to diffuse cheaply while still reconstructing sub-voxel material and geometric detail. Compared to keeping the full d-resolution grid, generating in d′ is what makes rectified-flow generation tractable at the reported inference times (Luce GS: 42.20s on H100).
  • Named contribution 3 — Multi-layer DINOv2 image conditioning.
    • Problem. Conditioning on a single deep DINOv2 layer gives good semantics but discards the early-layer spatial patterns (fine text, logos, inscriptions) that dense-prediction tasks — and, the paper argues, 3D generation — benefit from. Deep-only conditioning shows up as distorted or unreadable surface text on generated assets.
    • Mechanism. Features from multiple DINOv2 layers (in the reported configuration: layers 6, 12, 18, 24) are concatenated and projected into the conditioning space: h = Wproj[fℓ1; fℓ2; …; fℓL] + bproj. The projected features are injected through cross-attention into the SLatFlow DiT-style transformer that generates the SLatVAE latent, sample by sample, at every active voxel.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Early-layer DINOv2 features carry fine spatial structure; deep-layer features carry semantics. Fusing them lets the generator propagate high-frequency image detail through the flow to the rendered 3D surface. The ablation isolates the effect: multi-layer conditioning reaches Toys4K FID 20.99 and AI-image CLIP 0.8519, while single-layer (layer 24 only) gets 25.21 and 0.8081.
  • Named contribution 4 — Tangent-space normal map transfer.
    • Problem. Mesh extraction from a voxel grid bounds geometric frequency by the grid resolution, leaving sub-voxel surface features (engravings, fabric weaves, embossed text) unrepresented in the extracted geometry alone. Normal maps are the standard remedy in real-time rendering, but a generative pipeline that produces only meshes has no direct source for the normal-map texels.
    • Mechanism. Because Luce's normal Gaussians Gnor are decoupled from the mesh, they are supervised against the effective normals used for rendering — that is, the perturbed geometric normals after applying the authored normal map — so at training time they learn surface orientation at a frequency finer than the mesh can represent. At inference, the decoded normal Gaussians are expressed in the mesh's local tangent frame to produce a tangent-space normal map on the UV-parameterized surface. The final mesh carries four texture maps: diffuse (albedo), metallic, roughness, and tangent-space normal.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Tangent-space transfer preserves high-frequency surface detail at a resolution far beyond the mesh geometry, at zero polygon-count cost — mesh normal PSNR improves from 29.5 to 33.0 dB (Sec. 4.3). Prior GS-based methods tie per-Gaussian normals to the reconstructed surface (flattened-Gaussian shortest axis or depth-derived pseudo-normals), so they cannot carry sub-mesh normal detail; TexGaussian and TRELLIS 2 attach PBR attributes to per-vertex or per-voxel primitives with the same limitation.

Evaluation & Results

  • Training set: ~500K PBR-filtered assets from Objaverse and Objaverse-XL plus a 158K-asset PBR subset of TexVerse. Preprocessing renders each asset from 150 uniformly distributed cameras at 1024×1024; per-modality 3DGS is fit independently and voxelized onto a 1283 sparse grid with K = 8 Gaussians per voxel per modality.
  • Generation benchmarks: Toys4K (N = 412) with FID/KID on Inception and DINO backbones, plus CLIP, SigLIP2, ULIP, Uni3D-L alignment; and a 130-image benchmark of AI-generated images from Gemini 3.1 Flash Image, prompted to be diverse and detail-rich (text, logos, mixed materials).

Ablations

  • Multi-layer vs. single-layer DINOv2 conditioning. Layers 6/12/18/24 concatenated vs. layer 24 only: Toys4K FID 20.99 vs. 25.21; AI-image CLIP 0.8519 vs. 0.8081 (Appendix B). The multi-layer conditioning is what preserves legible surface text and logos in Fig. 4.
  • Tangent-space normal map transfer on/off. On the mesh path, tangent normal baking cuts FID from 25.04 to 21.10 and raises mesh normal PSNR from 29.5 to 33.0 dB — the mechanism for "high-frequency surface detail at zero polygon-count cost."
  • Per-modality decomposition. The qualitative comparison against TRELLIS 2's mesh-centric single-bundle PBR (Fig. 5) documents where the decomposition helps: distorted surface text on TRELLIS 2 / LiTo / TRELLIS becomes legible on Luce because albedo Gaussians can concentrate where woodgrain / print / logos need them without stealing from the normal or metallic-roughness modalities.
  • Efficiency envelope. Luce GS is the fastest sub-50s method that also leads FID; TRELLIS 2 at 7.48B parameters and 176.72s inference sits both larger and slower with worse FID (29.22) and CLIP.
