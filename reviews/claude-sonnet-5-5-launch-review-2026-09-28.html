Architecture

  • The launch post and system card do not disclose parameter count, context length as a headline number, attention variant, mixture-of-experts vs dense choice, or any low-level architecture detail. Anthropic continues its policy of not publishing architecture for Sonnet-tier and Opus-tier launches; the only architecture-adjacent fact stated is that Sonnet 5.5 inherits the "preserved thinking" feature expansion from Opus 5.5.
  • Named contribution — Preserved thinking (expanded).
    • Problem. Extended-reasoning models generate long chains of latent thinking tokens during a turn, but if those tokens are truncated or replaced with a summary between tool calls or between turns, the model has to re-derive the same intermediate conclusions on every subsequent call. On long agentic runs (Terminal-Bench, Cursor tasks, sustained OSWorld sessions) that amortization loss dominates cost.
    • Mechanism. Preserved thinking retains the raw thinking tokens across the boundary between tool responses and between turns within an agentic loop, feeding them back into the prefix of the next forward pass rather than discarding or summarizing them. Anthropic reports that Sonnet 5.5 "expands" this feature relative to Opus 5.5 but does not describe the specific delta — presumably a wider scope of preservation or lower guarding.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Discarding thinking between tool calls forces re-derivation and burns tokens the user pays for; summarizing loses the specific numeric or code-symbolic anchors the model relied on. Preserving verbatim keeps continuity but bloats context on long runs, which is the trade-off the "expansion" here is likely tuning. Anthropic ties the feature to the strong Terminal-Bench 4.0 and OSWorld 2.1 gains vs Sonnet 5.

Game Arena: Strategic LLM Evaluation in Competitive Environments

Tier 2 · Research Paper (Technical Report) arXiv 2026-09-25 (announced 2026-09-28) Evaluation · Game-theoretic ratings · Frontier model benchmarking

Overview

  • Ian Gemp, Marc Lanctot, Domino Weir, Elsa Dong, Daniel Hennes and colleagues at Google DeepMind and Google Research, with Will Cukierski and Meg Risdal from the Kaggle platform team (Kaggle is a Google subsidiary); arXiv:2609.31473v1 [cs.LG], submitted 2026-09-25 in the Monday 2026-09-28 announcement batch; 31 pages, 15 figures. Frontier-lab affiliation is satisfied by the Google DeepMind and Google Research author majority.
  • The paper documents the platform behind Kaggle Game Arena: a continuously-running head-to-head evaluation of frontier LLMs across three games chosen to be structurally different — Chess (perfect information, two-player), Poker (imperfect information with hidden hands), and Werewolf (multi-agent social deduction with private roles). The claim: static academic benchmarks saturate, and continuous game play scales evaluation difficulty with model strength.
  • Ten models are evaluated in a full round-robin: Gemini 3 Pro Preview, Gemini 3 Flash Preview, o3, GPT-5.2, GPT-5 mini, Grok 4, Grok 4.1 Fast Reasoning, Claude Opus 4.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Haiku 4.5 (Chess); DeepSeek V3.2 substitutes for Claude Haiku 4.5 in Poker. The line-up is one generation behind today's frontier (Claude 5.5 and GPT-6 are not measured), which the authors flag as intentional given the run duration.

Methodology

  • Named contribution — Text-based agent harness.
    • Problem. Cross-vendor LLM comparison in games has historically required custom action-space encoders per model or per game (chess piece indices vs FEN strings vs UCI vs board bitmaps). This couples the evaluation to the vendor's specific tool interfaces and makes the results incomparable when the harness changes.
    • Mechanism. Every model receives the game state and legal action set as natural-language text (FEN plus verbalized last moves for Chess; hand strings plus betting history for Poker; conversation transcript plus role prompt for Werewolf). Actions are returned as free-form text and parsed. This puts every model on the same wire format and lets the harness treat models as black boxes.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Any tokenizer difference across vendors is absorbed by the shared text protocol, so ranking differences trace to play strength, not encoder wins. The trade-off is that a strong model with a poor natural-language chess vocabulary will be underrated relative to a benchmark that feeds it FEN directly.
  • Named contribution — Game-Theoretic Evaluation (GTE) for Werewolf.
    • Problem. Werewolf outcomes depend heavily on role assignment (Werewolf vs Villager vs Seer); a model that is strong as a Werewolf but weak as a Villager may look average when averaged over roles, and worse, its rating will be dominated by whichever role happens to be sampled more often. Straight win-rate averages conflate role skill with role sampling.
    • Mechanism. GTE frames evaluation as a "model-vs-model-vs-role" meta-game with three strategic players. A Role Player chooses the game role to maximize the performance gap between Model A and Model B; Models A and B then play zero-sum within that role. The system solves for the maximum-entropy correlated equilibrium of the meta-game, and derives model ratings from the equilibrium marginal. This adversarially selects the roles that best discriminate between models, then rates using those discriminative matches.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Elo-style rating over uniform role sampling is dominated by roles where all models play similarly (low signal); GTE upweights the roles where models actually differ, extracting more information per match. Compared to prior LLM social-deduction benchmarks that report role-agnostic win rates, GTE surfaces role-specific asymmetries directly — and the paper shows exactly this: Gemini 3 Pro Preview improves any coalition it joins yet gets a negative individual rating in Werewolf.
  • Named contribution — Anti-saturation platform design.
    • Problem. Static benchmarks saturate once models solve them; refresh cycles are slow and each new dataset is released frozen.
    • Mechanism. Because the platform's opposition is the pool of models submitted to it, difficulty scales automatically with frontier strength. Model A's score against Model B updates in place whenever either model's play changes, and new game variants can be added without invalidating prior scores.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Kaggle competitions have used this dynamic for humans since 2010; Game Arena imports the mechanic wholesale for LLMs. Compared to Chatbot Arena's crowdsourced preference ratings, Game Arena's outcomes are objective ground truth from game rules rather than aggregated preference labels.

Ablations

  • Duplicate poker (each hand replayed with roles swapped) is the reported variance-reduction lever; the paper does not describe explicit ablations of harness choices, but the sample-size difference between Chess (40 games/pair) and Poker (20,000 hands/pair) exposes how much more Poker signal you need to separate models. The Werewolf format's ~31,500 games are similarly tuned for GTE stability at eight models.
  • Cross-game rank correlations are essentially zero for some pairs (Chess vs Poker), which the authors present as evidence for the anti-saturation-through-diversity claim: a leaderboard on any one game misses skills the others surface.

InternW0-Δ: A World Action Model Bridging Predictive Dynamics and Actions with 20K+ Hours of Open Data

Tier 2 · Research Paper (Technical Report) arXiv 2026-09-25 (announced 2026-09-28) Robotics · World action model · MoT · Video-action coupling

Overview

  • Physical Intelligence Team, Shanghai AI Laboratory; arXiv:2609.31394v1 [cs.RO], submitted 2026-09-25, 48+ authors including Xingyu Miao, Zizun Li, Baole Fang, Kaiwen Song, Tenghui Wang, Hanxue Zhang, Yating Wang, Xudong Li, Yuping He, Xueyuan Wei et al. Project page: internrobotics.github.io/InternW0-Delta. Frontier-lab affiliation is satisfied by the Shanghai AI Laboratory team majority.
  • InternW0-Δ is a direct follow-up to the InternW0 reviewed on 2026-09-24 (same author group, same MoT scaffolding). The Δ adds three named contributions on top of the InternW0 base: Causal Imprint for action-relevant predictive representations, 4D-aware representation distillation from a frozen Track4World teacher, and a sparse memory context (anchor / recent / current) that keeps the action-inference path light.
  • The paper positions itself against π0.5, DreamZero, Motus and Fast-WAM. The specific claim vs Fast-WAM is a shared design principle — separate future-video supervision from the action-inference path — but a different mechanism: Causal Imprint learns representations from training-only future supervision rather than distilling from a video generator, so inference-time has zero cost from the future-prediction branch.

Architecture

  • Named contribution — 30-block Mixture-of-Transformers with video and action experts.
    • Problem. Coupling video prediction and action generation naively (one shared trunk) forces the action head to spend capacity on visual dynamics it doesn't need, and forces the video expert to accept action-specific fine-tuning noise. The result is that the same model can be trained to be a good video model or a good policy, but not both.
    • Mechanism. Two pretrained experts — a video expert (visual dynamics, scene semantics, geometry) and an action expert (policy output) — are coupled through 30 directed Mixture-of-Transformers blocks. Each MoT block routes information in one direction (video → action) via cross-block attention, so gradients from the action loss cannot corrupt the video expert's representations, but the action expert can consume video features at every depth. The attention mask enforces this directionality per layer.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Directed MoT preserves the pretrained video expert's representation quality while giving the action expert layerwise access to it — a stronger version of the two-tower or adapter approaches, because information flow at every block is denser than at any single fusion point. Compared to InternW0's original MoT block (undirected), the directed variant explicitly prevents action-loss backpropagation into video features, which the authors flag as the reason for the improvement.
  • Named contribution — Causal Imprint.
    • Problem. Predictive video models help policies (Fast-WAM, DreamZero) but require running a video generator at inference time, which is prohibitively slow (>500 ms per action for full video rollout). If you skip the rollout, you lose the predictive signal entirely. And if you condition the policy on realized future frames during training (as some prior work does), you leak future information into the action head, so the model learns to expect ground-truth futures it will never have at deployment.
    • Mechanism. Causal Imprint aggregates recent and current visual features into a set of imprint tokens that predict adjacent latent differences (Δz) between successive frames. Supervision comes from actual future frames only during training: the imprint tokens are trained to encode "what should change next" without ever seeing the realized future. At inference, the imprint tokens are produced from the current observation alone and fed into the action expert as an auxiliary conditioning signal. No future frames are generated at deployment.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. The imprint captures "future-relevant scene changes" without requiring the model to synthesize pixels at inference — it distills the useful part of a full video generator (dynamics prediction) into a per-step feature that costs the same as any other token. Compared to Fast-WAM's separation of future-video supervision from action-inference, Causal Imprint further compresses the future-prediction signal from "a full generated frame" to "a small set of predictive tokens," explaining the reported 5.11× inference speedup.
  • Named contribution — 4D-aware representation distillation via frozen Track4World.
    • Problem. World action models trained on robot demonstrations tend to underweight geometry and motion (a 2D visual embedding suffices for many table-top tasks) and consequently generalize poorly to novel viewpoints or 3D configurations. Adding a full 3D scene understanding module inflates parameters and slows inference.
    • Mechanism. A frozen Track4World teacher (a pretrained 4D representation model, meaning 3D geometry over time) supplies geometry- and motion-aware feature targets. The InternW0-Δ video expert is trained with an auxiliary MSE loss against these teacher features. This is training-only: the teacher is discarded at inference, adding zero computational cost to policy execution.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Distillation from a specialist teacher is standard, but doing it against a 4D (spatio-temporal geometry) target on top of a 2D video backbone is what supplies the geometric prior the action expert relies on for novel-view generalization. Compared to end-to-end 3D-aware policies, this approach keeps the deployment model shape identical to a video/action MoT and pays only the training-time cost.
  • Named contribution — Sparse memory context (anchor / recent / current).
    • Problem. A dense history of observations grows quadratically with episode length in an attention-based action model, blowing up latency; but the naive alternative (only the current frame) discards the task-context signal that long-horizon manipulation needs.
    • Mechanism. The model maintains exactly three observation slots: the anchor (episode-start frame, encoding the initial task setup), the recent frame (immediately before the previous action chunk, encoding what just happened), and the current frame (present state). All three are fed to the video expert; the action expert consumes their MoT-routed representations.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. The three-frame budget is a much stronger signal than current-frame-only for phase-dependent tasks (e.g. "still bolting on the wheel" vs "already moved on to the next part"), and much cheaper than a sliding-window K observations. Compared to Fast-WAM's context, the explicit anchor slot supplies the task-setup signal that a rolling window loses after a few seconds.

Pre-training and training data

  • Total training corpus: over 20,000 hours after processing — the paper claims "the largest open-source corpus of its kind." The corpus integrates four heterogeneous sources:
    • Robot demonstrations. 15 datasets including AgiBotWorld, RoboMIND, Dexora, RH20T, RDT-1B, RoboSet.
    • Egocentric human data. EgoDex, EgoVerse. Aligned to robot format via action alignment, kinematic alignment, visual alignment, and action-speed alignment.
    • UMI data. Hy-UMI-10K.
    • Ego2Robot conversions. Human demonstrations retargeted to robot action space.
  • Data-processing pipeline: signal-anomaly and consistency filtering, static-boundary trimming, visual-quality filtering, action-magnitude guard, instruction-correctness verification. Filtered data indices are shipped with versioned references to the original samples so results are reproducible against the underlying open datasets.

The paper does not describe an RL post-training stage (this is behavior-cloning-heavy, in line with the π0-family lineage).

Highlight-Then-Summarize: Learning to Compress Evidence for Long-Context Understanding

Tier 2 · Research Paper arXiv 2026-09-25 (announced 2026-09-28) Long-context compression · RL · Evidence retrieval

Overview

  • Zhaoyuan Xia and Tong Mo (Peking University), Jianguang Jiang, Gaofeng Lu, Zhenyu Jiao, Xing Yuan, and Dai Dai (Baidu Inc.), Qinghongbing Xie, Yung Xiang Hue, and Long Zeng (Tsinghua University). arXiv:2609.31382v1 [cs.CL], submitted 2026-09-25, announced in the Monday 2026-09-28 batch. Baidu on the author list satisfies the frontier-lab filter.
  • The problem: long-context LLMs are asked to reason over 128K-token inputs where the relevant evidence is a small fraction, and forcing the model to attend to everything wastes generation budget on irrelevant content. The paper proposes a compress-then-reason pipeline: (1) highlight source-grounded question-relevant spans; (2) condense them into a compact question-conditioned summary; (3) answer from the summary.
  • Named artifacts: H2S-Dataset (6,647 training examples averaging 43.9K tokens of context, drawn from 11 benchmarks), H2S-RL (reinforcement-learning reward for evidence selection + summarization quality), H2S-Bench (seven-task evaluation suite), H2S-14B (the released model).

Methodology

  • Named contribution — Highlight-Then-Summarize (H2S) compress-then-reason paradigm.
    • Problem. A single-pass long-context reader has to allocate attention across the full input and generate the answer from that mixture. When evidence is sparse (a few paragraphs out of 128K tokens), most attention is wasted, and if the output budget is small (a few thousand tokens), the model may run out of budget before actually stating the answer. Simple retrieval + reader loses grounding because the reader can't verify claims against the source.
    • Mechanism. H2S is a two-stage prompt: stage 1 (Highlight) instructs the model to identify source-grounded question-relevant evidence spans as verbatim quotes; stage 2 (Summarize) has the same model rewrite those quotes into a compact question-conditioned summary; stage 3 (Answer) is a small final generation from the summary. Training is via H2S-RL: the reward decomposes into evidence-selection quality (does the highlighted span support the answer?) and summarization quality (is the summary faithful and covering?), avoiding a single opaque outcome-only signal.
    • Why it works / why it beats prior. Splitting the compression from the reasoning lets the output budget concentrate on the answer rather than on regurgitating irrelevant context, and the process-level rewards keep the highlight step from drifting toward hallucination. Compared to single-pass long-context reading, H2S-14B is smaller and cheaper; compared to retrieve-then-read (RAG) pipelines, the highlight stage stays inside the model's context so it can verify claims against the source in one pass.

Evaluation and results

  • Headline result: H2S-14B reaches an average score of 32.60 on H2S-Bench, outperforming Qwen3.8-27B by 10.17 points at roughly half the parameter count.
  • Performance retention under output-budget compression: 97.1% of the 16K-output baseline score is preserved when the output budget is cut to 4K tokens. This is the deployment-relevant metric: cheaper generation without meaningful accuracy loss.
  • Evidence-Summary Quality (the paper's own metric on selection + summarization faithfulness) is reported as highest among evaluated open-source models; specific numbers vs baselines are in figures not extractable as text here.
  • Context budget: 128K input tokens, 4K output tokens. Average context in the training set: 43.9K tokens across 6,647 examples drawn from 11 upstream benchmarks.
